# False Doctrine

The word "heresy" comes from the Greek word *hairesis*, from *haireomai*, meaning "I choose." In Scripture, heresy is not merely a mistake. It is a chosen departure from the faith once delivered to the saints (Jude 3). The word appears in places like Acts 24:5, 1 Corinthians 11:19, Galatians 5:20, and 2 Peter 2:1, carrying the sense of a faction, party, or teaching that separates itself from apostolic truth.

This section is a field guide, not a trophy case. False doctrine does not stay buried in old books. It changes clothes. It uses new names, humanitarian language, Bible study invitations, academic vocabulary, social justice slogans, self-help branding, prophetic language, mystical language, or restoration language. The old lie gets a fresh haircut and a nicer website.

The goal is not paranoia. The goal is discernment. Historic Christianity has always tested teaching by Scripture, especially on the essentials: who Jesus is, what the gospel is, what authority rules the church, whether repentance is preached, whether the cross is sufficient, and whether leaders produce good fruit or rotten fruit.

The list is not exhaustive. It is grouped by severity: movements that deny the gospel itself; movements that severely distort the gospel while keeping Christian vocabulary; and historic errors whose modern descendants still appear today. The order follows the working draft structure, not a claim that one entry is more alive, more dead, or more dangerous than another.

Each entry includes alternate names, front groups, and common identifiers where they matter. Recognition is the first defense against deception. A movement that recruits under one name and reveals its doctrine under another is already operating by the pattern Paul warned about in 2 Corinthians 11:13-14: false apostles disguising themselves as servants of righteousness.

Test every teaching, including this one, by Scripture (Acts 17:11, 1 Thessalonians 5:21, 1 John 4:1). The Holy Spirit was not given so intermediaries could stand between you and the Scriptures. He was given so you could open it yourself and understand. Read the cited passages. Read them in context. Do not accept any human writer's framing without verification.

## Fast Recognition Guide

False doctrine usually announces itself through one or more of these patterns:

1. **Another Jesus.** Jesus becomes a created being, an angel, a mere prophet, a divine idea, a temporary mode of God, a spirit brother of Lucifer, a failed messiah, or one incarnation among many.

2. **Another gospel.** Salvation becomes Christ plus membership, Christ plus ritual, Christ plus secret knowledge, Christ plus a prophet, Christ plus a church office, Christ plus money, Christ plus works, Christ plus ethnic identity, Christ plus law keeping, or Christ plus obedience to a human organization.

3. **Another authority.** Scripture becomes useful but not final. A prophet, magisterium, governing body, seer, book, vision, inner voice, apostle, guru, council, or academic elite gets the final word.

4. **Secret knowledge.** The movement says ordinary believers have missed the real truth. Only the inner circle, restored church, enlightened teacher, hidden text, lost gospel, coded prophecy, or special revelation can unlock it.

5. **Deceptive recruitment.** The group hides behind "Bible study," "volunteer work," "free classes," "community service," "campus fellowship," "spiritual coaching," or "interfaith dialogue" before giving its real name and doctrine.

6. **Fear-based control.** People are told they cannot leave without losing salvation, family, identity, community, or divine protection.

7. **Bad fruit protected by spiritual language.** Money abuse, sexual abuse, family alienation, authoritarian control, failed prophecies, and coverups get defended as "persecution," "submission," "spiritual warfare," or "touch not the Lord's anointed."

8. **Words are redefined.** Terms like Jesus, grace, gospel, resurrection, salvation, repentance, sin, spirit, church, love, and truth are kept, but the meanings are changed under the hood.

The names change. The pattern does not.

## Tier One: Movements That Deny the Gospel Itself

These movements either deny the deity of Christ, deny the sufficiency of His atonement, add new revelations equal or superior to Scripture, or present a different Jesus and a different gospel from the one the apostles preached. Galatians 1:8-9 calls such teachings *anathema*, accursed.

### World Mission Society Church of God

**Also known as:** WMSCOG. Church of God. Church of God World Mission Society. Iglesia de Dios Sociedad Misionera Mundial. Hananim Eomeoni Gyohoe, Korean for "God the Mother Church". The Watv Church. Often recruits under generic names: Bible study group, ASEZ ("Save the Earth from A to Z"), ASEZ WAO, We Love U Foundation, Mother's Street, Church of God volunteer organization. Front events have been documented under names like "Pass It On" and various clean-up or charity events that conceal religious affiliation until members are recruited.

**Founders:** Ahn Sahng-hong (1918-1985, South Korea) and Zahng Gil-jah (born 1943).

**Core claim:** Ahn Sahng-hong was the second coming of Jesus Christ. Zahng Gil-jah ("Mother Jerusalem," "God the Mother," "Heavenly Mother") is the bride of Christ from Revelation, a divine female counterpart to the Father. Salvation requires acknowledging both.

**Recruitment tactics:** Documented in court filings, journalism (CBN, Christianity Today, KBS Korea), and former-member testimony. Approaches typically begin with a young, attractive recruiter on a college campus inviting a "free Bible study," "free English class," or "volunteer opportunity" without disclosing the group's name or doctrine. The "God the Mother" doctrine is revealed only after the prospect is emotionally invested. Multiple U.S. lawsuits since 2011 have alleged labor exploitation, undisclosed proselytizing, and family alienation. Korean media has documented similar patterns since the 1990s.

**Why it is heresy:**

- Adds a second messiah: (Matthew 24:23-27) explicitly warns against this. "Behold, I have told you beforehand. So, if they say to you, 'Look, he is in the wilderness,' do not go out... For as the lightning comes from the east and shines as far as the west, so will be the coming of the Son of Man."
- Adds "God the Mother" to the Godhead: There is no Hebrew or Greek warrant for this. The "bride" of Revelation 21:9 is the church (Ephesians 5:25-32), not a divine female counterpart to the Father.
- Christ's atoning work was once for all: (Hebrews 9:26-28, Hebrews 10:12-14). A second messiah is theologically unnecessary because the first one's work was complete.
- The Father is the only proper name for God in the New Testament prayer model: (Matthew 6:9), and Jesus reveals the Father, not a Mother (John 14:9).

### Peoples Temple

**Also known as:** Peoples Temple of the Disciples of Christ. Peoples Temple Christian Church Full Gospel. Peoples Temple Apostolic Church. Peoples Temple Agricultural Project (the Guyana commune name). Jonestown (the colloquial name for the Guyana settlement). The original full corporate name when affiliated with the Disciples of Christ denomination was "Peoples Temple of the Disciples of Christ"; the affiliation was nominal cover.

**Founder:** Jim Jones, 1955, originally in Indianapolis, later San Francisco and Guyana.

**Outcome:** On November 18, 1978, Jones led 909 followers, including 304 children, in mass murder-suicide by cyanide-laced Flavor Aid at the Jonestown compound in Guyana, after his men ambushed and killed U.S. Congressman Leo Ryan and four others at a nearby airstrip. It was the largest single-day loss of American civilian life until September 11, 2001.

**Doctrinal trajectory:** Jones started as a nominal Pentecostal preacher and progressively replaced biblical Christianity with what he called "Apostolic Socialism." By the 1970s he openly denied the Bible, denied a personal God, and presented himself as the reincarnation of Jesus, Buddha, Lenin, and others. The "Christianity" of Peoples Temple was a facade for a personality cult that had abandoned Scripture entirely.

**Why it is in this list:** Peoples Temple is the historical reminder of where charismatic leadership without scriptural accountability leads. (Matthew 7:15-20): "Beware of false prophets, who come to you in sheep's clothing but inwardly are ravenous wolves. You will recognize them by their fruits."

### The Family International

**Also known as:** TFI. Children of God (the original name, 1968-1978). Family of Love (1978-1982). The Family (1982-2004). The Family International (2004-present). Music with Meaning. Heaven's Magic. The Childcare Action Project (an early front). The "Mo Letters" or "Mo Books" are the writings of founder David Berg, often distributed as standalone tracts. Sub-organizations and affiliated outreach names have included Activated Ministries, Aurora Production AG, and various local "Family Care" or "Family Help" charity-style names.

**Founder:** David Berg ("Moses David," "Father David," "Mo," "King David"), 1968 in Huntington Beach, California.

**Core distortions:** Sexualization of evangelism; adult-on-adult and, documentedly, adult-on-minor sexual abuse; apocalyptic communalism.

**Documented abuses:**

- "Flirty Fishing" (1974-1987): Female members were instructed to use sexual seduction to attract converts. Berg's published "Mo Letters" explicitly directed this. The group's own internal records report over 100,000 men "won" through this method.
- Child sexual abuse: Multiple government investigations (United Kingdom, Argentina, France, Australia) and the testimony of hundreds of former members born into the group document widespread abuse of children. Berg's own writings ("The Story of Davidito," 1982) sexualized minors. A 1995 UK High Court judgment (Ward J., re ST) found the group had "promoted and engaged in sexual activities with children."
- Suicides of children raised in the group: Ricky Rodriguez ("Davidito"), the subject of Berg's writings, killed his former nanny and then himself in 2005. Multiple other suicides of TFI-raised children have been documented.

**Why it is heresy:** Beyond doctrinal error, the documented sexual exploitation of children violates (Matthew 18:6): "Whoever causes one of these little ones who believe in me to sin, it would be better for him to have a great millstone fastened around his neck and to be drowned in the depth of the sea." (1 Timothy 4:1-3) describes "doctrines of demons" advanced by those whose consciences are seared. The Family International is one of the clearest modern examples.

The "restorationist" claim that the gospel was lost between the apostles and the founder is contradicted by the continuous witness of Christian martyrs, manuscripts, and writings from the second century onward. (Matthew 16:18): "I will build my church, and the gates of hell shall not prevail against it."

### Jehovah's Witnesses / Watch Tower Bible and Tract Society

**Also known as:** JW. JWs. Witnesses. Russellites (early name, after founder Charles Taze Russell). Bible Students (the pre-1931 name). International Bible Students Association (IBSA). Watch Tower Bible and Tract Society of Pennsylvania (the legal corporate entity). Watchtower Bible and Tract Society of New York. Christian Congregation of Jehovah's Witnesses. The Governing Body (the eight-man leadership council based in Warwick, NY). Kingdom Hall (their meeting place name). The Watchtower and Awake! magazines are their flagship publications. *jw.org* is the official web presence.

**Founder:** Charles Taze Russell, 1879. Reorganized under Joseph Franklin Rutherford in the 1930s. Currently headquartered in Warwick, New York.

**Core distortions:** Modern Arianism plus a unique end-times framework plus institutional control via a centralized Governing Body.

**Specific teachings:**

- Jesus is Michael the Archangel, a created being, not eternally divine.
- The Holy Spirit is an impersonal force, not a person of the Godhead.
- Salvation requires affiliation with the Watch Tower organization. Outside the organization, no salvation.
- Only 144,000 (the "anointed class") go to heaven. The rest of the faithful inherit a paradise earth.
- Blood transfusions are forbidden, even when life-threatening, based on a misreading of Acts 15:29.
- Birthdays and most holidays (Christmas, Easter, Thanksgiving) are forbidden as pagan.
- Disfellowshipping (their formal shunning practice) of members who leave or are expelled. Documented to include cutting off contact with immediate family members, including spouses and parents.

**Historical racial teaching:** Early Watch Tower publications also repeated racial ideas tied to the old "curse of Ham" or "curse of Canaan" interpretation. Some early material connected Black people with Noah's curse, spoke of servitude as part of that curse, and even speculated about Black skin being changed in the restored world. Modern Jehovah's Witness publications now reject this interpretation and correctly state that Genesis curses Canaan, not Ham, and gives no biblical basis for treating Black people as cursed. The point is not that every current Jehovah's Witness believes this today; many do not. The point is that the organization has a documented history of publishing racial teaching it later abandoned, which fits its broader pattern of changing doctrine while still claiming unique prophetic authority.

**The New World Translation:** The Watch Tower's official Bible translation, first published 1950-1961. The translation committee was anonymous, and most translators have since been identified as not having formal training in biblical Hebrew or Greek. The NWT systematically alters texts that affirm the deity of Christ. The most famous example is John 1:1, which the NWT renders "the Word was a god" rather than "the Word was God." This translation is rejected by every recognized Greek scholarship resource because the Greek grammar (Colwell's Rule, anarthrous predicate nominatives preceding the verb) does not permit it.

**Failed prophecies (a partial list):**

- 1914: Russell predicted the visible return of Christ and the end of Gentile times. When 1914 passed, the doctrine was retroactively revised to claim Christ had returned "invisibly."
- 1918, 1925, 1941, 1975: Each was predicted as the end. Each passed.
- The 1975 failure was particularly damaging; many members sold homes and quit jobs. When it passed, the organization blamed members for "misunderstanding."

Deuteronomy 18:22 is direct: "When a prophet speaks in the name of the LORD, if the word does not come to pass or come true, that is a word that the LORD has not spoken; the prophet has spoken it presumptuously. You need not be afraid of him."

**Why it is heresy:** Denies the deity of Christ (John 1:1, John 20:28, Colossians 2:9). Adds the Watch Tower organization as a mediator between God and man, contradicting 1 Timothy 2:5. Creates two classes of believers, contradicting Galatians 3:28 and the universal language of "all who call on the name of the Lord" (Romans 10:13). Replaces the bodily resurrection of Christ (Luke 24:39) with a spiritual resurrection.

### Mormonism / The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints

**Also known as:** LDS. LDS Church. The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints. Latter-day Saints. Saints (member self-reference). Mormons. The Brethren (referring to top leadership). The Quorum of the Twelve Apostles. The First Presidency. The Brighamites (historical, distinguishing the Utah church). The "Brighton Young Church" (informal historical reference). Affiliated and breakaway groups include the Community of Christ (formerly Reorganized LDS Church, RLDS, headquartered in Independence, MO; significantly more mainstream Christian than the Utah LDS), the Fundamentalist Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-Day Saints (FLDS, the polygamist breakaway under Warren Jeffs), the Apostolic United Brethren (AUB), and the Kingston Group (Davis County Cooperative Society). The mainstream Utah-based church has, since 2018 under President Russell M. Nelson, been pushing back against the "Mormon" nickname and asking media to use the full church name.

**Founder:** Joseph Smith Jr., 1830. Founded in Fayette, New York, after Smith claimed an angel named Moroni led him to gold plates buried near Palmyra, NY, which he then translated into the Book of Mormon using "seer stones" placed in a hat.

**Core distortions:** Adds new scriptures equal to or above the Bible. Redefines God. Teaches that humans can become gods.

**Specific teachings:**

- "As man now is, God once was; as God now is, man may become." (Lorenzo Snow, fifth President of the LDS Church, 1840). This doctrine, called eternal progression or exaltation, teaches that God the Father was once a mortal man on another planet who progressed to godhood, and that faithful Mormon men can do the same.
- God the Father has a physical body of flesh and bone (Doctrine and Covenants 130:22).
- There are many gods, not one. This is plural exaltation, not the henotheism of polytheism, but it is not biblical monotheism either.
- Jesus and Lucifer are spirit-brothers, both sons of Heavenly Father.
- The Book of Mormon, Doctrine and Covenants, and Pearl of Great Price are scriptures equal to or above the Bible. The Bible is "translated correctly" only insofar as it agrees with these.
- Salvation requires faith in Christ plus baptism, plus temple ordinances, plus continued obedience, plus (for the highest exaltation) celestial marriage.

**The Curse of Ham and Mormon racism:** The LDS Church taught that black men of African descent were under a divine curse and were forbidden from holding the priesthood. The teaching drew on a misreading of Genesis 9:25 (the "curse of Ham," who was actually never cursed; Canaan was) and on Mormon-specific scriptures like the Book of Abraham, which described black skin as a mark of divine disfavor. In 1978, LDS President Spencer W. Kimball announced a new revelation reversing this. The reversal was presented as new revelation rather than as repentance for prior error. The "Curse of Ham" misreading was also widely used by Southern Baptists and other Protestant denominations to justify American slavery; this is not unique to Mormonism, but the Mormon application persisted longer institutionally. But the church did not clearly disavow the old racist explanations until 2013, when it rejected past theories that Black skin was a sign of divine disfavor, curse, premortal unrighteousness, interracial marriage as sin, or racial inferiority.

**The Book of Mormon's archaeological problem:** The Book of Mormon describes a Hebrew civilization in pre-Columbian America with horses, chariots, wheat, barley, steel swords, and elephants, none of which existed in pre-Columbian America. No archaeological evidence supports any city, battle, or civilization described in it. DNA studies of Native American populations show Asian descent, not Hebrew, contradicting the Book of Mormon's claim that Native Americans descend from a lost tribe of Israel.

**Joseph Smith's character:** Smith practiced polygamy secretly while publicly denying it; documented evidence shows at least 33 wives, several of them already married to other men, several as young as 14. He was killed in 1844 in a gunfight at Carthage Jail, Illinois, where he was being held on charges of inciting a riot after destroying a printing press that had exposed his polygamy. Mormon historians acknowledge most of these facts; LDS apologists frame them sympathetically.

**Why it is heresy:**

- Denies the eternality and uniqueness of God: (Isaiah 43:10): "Before me no god was formed, nor shall there be any after me." (Isaiah 44:6): "I am the first and I am the last; besides me there is no god."
- Adds to Scripture: (Revelation 22:18-19) and (Proverbs 30:6) directly forbid this.
- Presents a different Jesus: (2 Corinthians 11:4) warns of those who preach "another Jesus" and "a different gospel."
- Salvation by works added to faith: (Galatians 2:16, Ephesians 2:8-9, Titus 3:5).

### Arianism

**Also known as:** Arian Christianity. Anti-Nicene theology. Subordinationism (in milder forms). Semi-Arianism (Homoiousian: the Son is "of similar substance" rather than "of the same substance"). Eunomianism (after Eunomius, an extreme Arian). The "Homoian" position (Son is "like" the Father). Modern descendants in this list (Jehovah's Witnesses, Iglesia ni Cristo, Christadelphians, some Unitarian groups) preserve the Arian Christology under different organizational names.

**Founder:** Arius, a presbyter in Alexandria, Egypt, c. 318-336 AD.

**Core claim:** Jesus is a created being. He is the highest of all creatures, made by the Father before time, but He is not eternally God. Arius's slogan was *en pote hote ouk en*, "there was a time when He was not."

**Why it is heresy:** It denies the eternal deity of the Son. If the Son is created, He cannot be the Creator (John 1:3, Colossians 1:16, Hebrews 1:2). If the Son is not eternally God, He cannot reveal the Father (John 1:18). If the Son is a creature, worship of Him is idolatry (Exodus 20:3-5), yet Scripture commands the worship of the Son (Hebrews 1:6, Philippians 2:10-11, Revelation 5:13-14).

**Scriptural conflicts:**

- (John 1:1): "In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God." The Greek *theos en ho logos* is unambiguous; the Word was God, eternally.
- (John 8:58): "Before Abraham was, I am." Yeshua applies the divine name *ego eimi* (the Septuagint rendering of *ehyeh asher ehyeh* in Exodus 3:14) to Himself.
- (Colossians 1:15-17): "He is the image of the invisible God, the firstborn of all creation. For by him all things were created... all things were created through him and for him. And he is before all things, and in him all things hold together." *Prototokos* (firstborn) here means preeminent in rank, not first in time; the rest of the passage rules out any reading of Him as a creature.
- (Colossians 2:9): "For in him the whole fulness of deity dwells bodily."
- (Hebrews 1:8): "But of the Son he says, 'Your throne, O God, is forever and ever.'" The Father directly calls the Son God.
- (Titus 2:13): "...our great God and Savior Jesus Christ."

**Historical record:** The Council of Nicaea convened in 325 AD specifically to address Arianism. Of the approximately 318 bishops present, all but two signed the Nicene Creed declaring the Son *homoousios*, of one substance with the Father. The two who refused were exiled along with Arius. Arius died in Constantinople in 336 AD under disputed circumstances.

**Modern descendants:** Arianism survives today in the Jehovah's Witnesses, Iglesia ni Cristo, Christadelphians, and most Unitarian movements. Whenever a teacher claims Jesus is "a god" rather than "the God," or denies that He is co-eternal with the Father, that is Arianism by another name.

## Tier Two: Movements That Severely Distort the Gospel

These movements use Christian vocabulary and may affirm certain Christian doctrines, but they fundamentally distort the gospel by adding requirements beyond faith in Christ, by replacing biblical authority with tradition or new revelation, or by promoting practices Scripture forbids.

---

### Affirming Churches (gospel-distorting forms)

**Also known as:** Affirming Christianity. Open and Affirming (UCC term). Reconciling Ministries (United Methodist term). Welcoming and Affirming (American Baptist term). More Light Presbyterians. Inclusive Church. Progressive Christianity (overlapping but broader category). Q Christian Fellowship. The Reformation Project (Matthew Vines's organization). Generous Space (Canadian). House for All Sinners and Saints (Nadia Bolz-Weber's congregation, ELCA-affiliated). Many local churches simply call themselves "affirming" or "inclusive" without a denominational tag.

**Note:** "Affirming" in this context means churches that affirm same-sex sexual relationships, gender transition, and broader sexual practice as compatible with Christian discipleship, often by reinterpreting or dismissing the biblical texts on sexuality.

This entry is about institutional affirmation, not about how individual believers wrestle with same-sex attraction or gender dysphoria. A church can love and welcome anyone struggling with any sin (which is what every church is for); it cannot redefine sin without redefining the gospel.

**Core distortion:** Reinterprets (Romans 1:26-27, 1 Corinthians 6:9-11, 1 Timothy 1:9-11, Leviticus 18:22) to mean something other than what they have meant for two millennia of Jewish and Christian readers. The reinterpretation typically claims:

- Romans 1 condemns only "exploitative" same-sex acts, not "loving" ones.
- 1 Corinthians 6:9 *malakoi* and *arsenokoitai* refer only to specific practices like temple prostitution.
- Old Testament prohibitions are obsolete or culturally bound.

The Greek and Hebrew do not bear these reinterpretations. *Arsenokoitai* (1 Corinthians 6:9, 1 Timothy 1:10) is constructed from the Septuagint of Leviticus 18:22 and 20:13 (*arsenos koiten*), making the connection deliberate. The text condemns the act, not the context. Romans 1's language describes mutual desire (*orexei*) between same-sex partners, not exploitation.

**Why it is heresy:**

- It is not the reinterpretation alone but the combination: a clear scriptural prohibition is reinterpreted to fit cultural pressure, the reinterpretation cannot be sustained from the text, and the reinterpretation is then made the test of orthodoxy. The pattern is identical to liberal denominations rewriting the deity of Christ in the 20th century.
- (1 Corinthians 6:9-11) is direct: "Or do you not know that the unrighteous will not inherit the kingdom of God? Do not be deceived: neither the sexually immoral, nor idolaters, nor adulterers, nor men who practice homosexuality, nor thieves, nor the greedy, nor drunkards, nor revilers, nor swindlers will inherit the kingdom of God. **And such were some of you. But you were washed, you were sanctified, you were justified** in the name of the Lord Jesus Christ and by the Spirit of our God." The gospel is that people change. To affirm what Scripture calls sin as compatible with Christian identity is to deny the transforming work of the Spirit (verse 11), which is to deny the gospel.
- The same logic is applied across the list of sins: greed, drunkenness, theft. No church would claim a "thief-affirming" identity. The selective application reveals the cultural rather than scriptural source of the doctrine.

### Prosperity Gospel and Word of Faith Movement

**Also known as:** Word of Faith. Word-Faith. WoF. Health-and-Wealth Gospel. Name-It-and-Claim-It. Positive Confession. Faith Movement. Seed-Faith (Oral Roberts's terminology). Hour of Power (Robert Schuller's broadcast lineage, softer variant). The "Latter Rain" movement (overlapping). New Apostolic Reformation (NAR, the C. Peter Wagner network, heavily overlapping). The Bethel Movement (Bill Johnson, Bethel Church in Redding, CA). Hillsong (Australian megachurch with prosperity-adjacent theology). Lakewood Church (Joel Osteen). Without Walls International Church. Trinity Broadcasting Network (TBN, the primary broadcast vehicle for the movement).

**Founders/popularizers:** E.W. Kenyon (foundational early figure). Kenneth Hagin. Kenneth Copeland. Oral Roberts. Benny Hinn. Joel Osteen. Creflo Dollar. Paula White. Joyce Meyer. T.D. Jakes (with reservations; he is harder to categorize). Jesse Duplantis. Eddie Long. Bill Johnson. Heidi and Rolland Baker. Reinhard Bonnke (more evangelistic, Word-of-Faith influenced).

**Core teaching:** God wants every believer wealthy and healthy. Sufficient faith (or sufficient seed-faith offerings to a ministry) guarantees these outcomes. Sickness and poverty are signs of insufficient faith. If someone is sick, poor, anxious, infertile, depressed, or suffering, the blame is often placed on weak faith, negative speech, hidden sin, generational curses, or failure to sow money into the right ministry.

This is not biblical faith. It is baptized greed.

**Why it is heresy:** It treats God as a vending machine. It exploits poor and desperate people. It contradicts every biblical text on suffering (Romans 5:3-5, James 1:2-4, 1 Peter 4:12-13, 2 Corinthians 12:7-10). It contradicts the example of every apostle, all of whom died poor and most violently. It produces ministries with private jets while their donors live in poverty. (1 Timothy 6:5): "imagining that godliness is a means of gain."

**Common phrases to recognize:**

- "Sow a seed."
- "Name it and claim it."
- "Speak it into existence."
- "Your words create your reality."
- "You are blocking your blessing."
- "You need to activate your miracle."
- "Money is coming to you."
- "God wants you rich."
- "Poverty is a curse."
- "Sickness is never God's will."
- "If you had enough faith, you would be healed."
- "Touch not the Lord's anointed."
- "The wealth of the wicked is laid up for the righteous."
- "Give your best seed for your breakthrough."

**Scriptural correction:**

- Jesus had nowhere to lay His head (Matthew 8:20).
- The apostles were beaten, imprisoned, hungry, poor, and killed (1 Corinthians 4:9-13, 2 Corinthians 11:23-28).
- Paul learned contentment in abundance and need (Philippians 4:11-13).
- Godliness is not a means of gain (1 Timothy 6:5).
- The love of money is a root of all kinds of evil (1 Timothy 6:10).
- Jesus warned that riches can choke the word (Mark 4:19).
- Christians are promised suffering, not luxury (John 16:33, 2 Timothy 3:12).
- Paul was not healed after praying three times; God's grace was sufficient (2 Corinthians 12:7-10).

**Why it is dangerous:** Prosperity preaching preys on the desperate. The sick give money because they want healing. The poor give money because they want provision. The grieving give money because they want hope. Then, when the miracle does not come, the preacher keeps the money and the victim keeps the shame.

This is why Scripture speaks so sharply about greedy teachers. (2 Peter 2:3): "And in their greed they will exploit you with false words."

**The answer:** Biblical giving is worship, not a slot machine. Biblical faith trusts God even when healing does not come. Biblical blessing may include provision, but it never turns Christ into a servant of human appetite. The true gospel says Christ is enough in poverty, sickness, suffering, prison, weakness, and death. Any preacher who cannot preach that gospel to a hospital room, a prison cell, and a graveside is not preaching the gospel of Jesus Christ.

### New Apostolic Reformation and Modern Apostle Movements

**Also known as:** NAR. Apostolic-prophetic movement. Fivefold ministry restoration. Modern apostles and prophets. Kingdom Now. Dominion theology. Seven Mountain Mandate. Apostolic covering. Prophetic alignment. Bethel movement overlap. IHOPKC overlap. Wagner movement. Signs and wonders movement. Third Wave charismatic movement.

**Core teaching:** God is supposedly restoring governing apostles and prophets to lead the end-time church. These leaders claim special authority, fresh revelation, prophetic insight, supernatural impartation, and strategic dominion over culture.

**Common phrases to recognize:**

- "Apostolic covering."
- "Prophetic alignment."
- "Fresh revelation."
- "New wine."
- "The next move of God."
- "Touch not the Lord's anointed."
- "Activate your mantle."
- "Shift the atmosphere."
- "Take the seven mountains."

**Historical roots:** Modalism is ancient. It was taught by Sabellius (early third century), for whom it is also called Sabellianism. Tertullian wrote against it as early as 213 AD. The early church rejected it as heresy because it denied real distinction within the Godhead.

**Modern Oneness emerged:** From a 1913 camp meeting in Arroyo Seco, California, where R.E. **The "Jesus' name only" baptism:** Oneness churches teach that valid baptism must be in the name of "Jesus" only (citing Acts 2:38, Acts 8:16, Acts 19:5), and that Matthew 28:19 ("in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit") refers to Jesus alone, since Jesus is supposedly all three. They re-baptize converts from Trinitarian churches.

**Why it is heresy:**

- Father, Son, and Spirit are distinct persons in clear, simultaneous interaction throughout Scripture: (Matthew 3:16-17, the baptism of Jesus, with the Father speaking from heaven, the Son being baptized, the Spirit descending as a dove). This is impossible if they are the same Person playing three roles.
- (John 17): Jesus prays to the Father. He does not pray to Himself. The high priestly prayer makes no sense in modalism.
- (John 14:16): Jesus says He will ask the Father to send another Helper. The Greek *allos* (another of the same kind) presupposes distinction.
- (Hebrews 1:8-9): The Father directly addresses the Son as God and as a distinct person.
- (Matthew 28:19): "the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit" (singular "name," but three articulated persons: *tou Patros kai tou Huiou kai tou Hagiou Pneumatos*). The Greek grammar makes the three distinct.

**Practical consequence:** Oneness theology denies the eternal love within the Godhead. If God is one Person, He had no one to love until He created. Trinitarian theology grounds love eternally in God Himself: the Father loves the Son in the Spirit from before time. (1 John 4:8): "God is love." This is meaningful only if God is internally relational.

### Roman Catholic Idolatry, Veneration, and Indulgences

**Also known as:** Roman Catholic Church. RCC. Catholic Church. The Holy See. The Vatican. Latin Church (the largest sui iuris particular church within Catholicism). Eastern Catholic Churches (24 self-governing churches in communion with Rome; theologically Catholic, liturgically diverse). Specific religious orders that often appear in evangelistic or institutional contexts: Society of Jesus (Jesuits, SJ). Order of Preachers (Dominicans, OP). Order of Friars Minor (Franciscans, OFM). Opus Dei (Prelature of the Holy Cross and Opus Dei, a personal prelature). Legionaries of Christ (LC). Knights of Columbus (lay fraternal order). Catholic Charities. Catholic Relief Services. EWTN (Eternal Word Television Network, the largest Catholic broadcasting network). Specific Marian apparition cults: Our Lady of Guadalupe, Our Lady of Fatima, Our Lady of Lourdes, Our Lady of Medjugorje (the last is unapproved by the Vatican but widely venerated).

The Roman Catholic Church is a complex institution. It affirms the Trinity, the deity of Christ, the bodily resurrection, and the authority of much of Scripture. It also adds practices and doctrines that historic Protestantism, the Eastern Orthodox tradition (in some particulars), and the apostolic writings themselves contradict. This entry addresses the specific distortions, not the entire tradition.

**Veneration of Mary and the saints:** Catholic doctrine distinguishes between *latria* (worship due only to God), *hyperdulia* (the veneration given to Mary), and *dulia* (veneration given to other saints). In practice, the distinction collapses. Mary is prayed to, addressed as "Co-Redemptrix" and "Mediatrix of all graces" in Catholic devotional literature, and credited with miraculous interventions. The Hail Mary prayer asks her to "pray for us sinners now and at the hour of our death," presupposing she hears prayers from millions of people simultaneously, an attribute of God alone. No where in Scripture does it say to pray to Mary.

Scripture is direct:

- (1 Timothy 2:5): "For there is one God, and there is one mediator between God and men, the man Christ Jesus."
- (Exodus 20:3-5): "You shall have no other gods before me. You shall not make for yourself a carved image... You shall not bow down to them or serve them."
- (Deuteronomy 18:11): The dead are not to be consulted; necromancy is forbidden. Praying to deceased saints is functionally indistinguishable from this.
- (Matthew 6:9): The Father is the proper recipient of prayer.

**Indulgences:** Originally penances for sin in this life, indulgences became, by the late medieval period, payments to the church that would reduce time in purgatory for oneself or a deceased loved one. Johann Tetzel's slogan, "When the coin in the coffer rings, the soul from purgatory springs," is documented (sometimes paraphrased; the substance is preserved in the records of Tetzel's preaching tours, 1517). Tetzel was a Dominican friar selling indulgences in Germany on behalf of Pope Leo X to fund the rebuilding of St. Peter's Basilica. Martin Luther's 95 Theses, posted October 31, 1517, were a direct response. Indulgences as a paid transaction were officially curbed at the Council of Trent (1545-1563), but the underlying theology of purgatory and indulgences remains in Catholic doctrine.

Scripture has no concept of purgatory. (Hebrews 9:27): "It is appointed for man to die once, and after that comes judgment." (Luke 23:43): "Today you will be with me in paradise." Christ's atonement is sufficient (Hebrews 10:14): "For by a single offering he has perfected for all time those who are being sanctified." Adding to it with payments, masses, or temporal penances is the same error Galatians 3:1-5 confronts.

**Confession to a priest:** Catholic doctrine requires confession of mortal sins to a priest for absolution. James 5:16 says "confess your sins to one another" (mutual, not clerical), and 1 John 1:9 directs confession to God: "If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins." The institutional confessional, with its accumulation of intimate knowledge in the hands of a clerical class, has been documented as a tool of social control for centuries (see, for example, the Spanish Inquisition's use of confessional records).

**Antipopes and historical instability:** The Catholic Church recognizes that there have been multiple antipopes throughout history, men who claimed the papacy in opposition to the recognized Bishop of Rome. The Western Schism (1378-1417) had three claimants simultaneously, each excommunicating the others. The Avignon Papacy (1309-1376) saw the papacy moved to France under political control of the French monarchy. The historical claim that the papacy is an unbroken apostolic succession with continuous spiritual authority is hard to reconcile with these documented periods of confusion, corruption, and violent succession disputes. Pope Stephen VI literally exhumed the corpse of Pope Formosus in 897 AD, dressed it in papal vestments, and put it on trial (the "Cadaver Synod") before declaring his decrees invalid.

**The new Modernism:** Within contemporary Catholicism, theological liberalism (often labeled "Modernism" by traditional Catholics) increasingly accepts evolutionary cosmology unmoored from biblical Genesis, downplays the historicity of biblical events, promotes interfaith universalism (the idea that sincere followers of any religion may be saved), and softens or rewrites traditional moral teaching on sexuality. This is a separate critique from the medieval distortions above; it is the modern Catholic intellectual class drifting from even the institution's own historic teaching.

**The honest summary:** Roman Catholicism contains genuine Christians who trust Christ for salvation. The institutional doctrines surrounding Mary, the saints, indulgences, purgatory, the priesthood, and papal authority systematically obscure the sufficiency of Christ's finished work. (Galatians 1:6-9) describes the seriousness of "a different gospel"; the question for any Catholic reader is whether the gospel they have received is the one Paul preached.

### Islam

**Also known as:** Islam. The Religion of Submission. Mohammedanism (an obsolete and now-rejected Western term). Sunni Islam (the largest branch, ~85-90% of Muslims). Shia Islam (the second-largest branch, predominant in Iran, Iraq, Bahrain, Lebanon). Twelver Shia (Ithna Ashariyyah, the largest Shia subgroup). Isma'ili Shia (including the Nizari Ismaili community led by the Aga Khan). Alawites (Syria; an esoteric Shia offshoot, theologically further from mainstream Shia than Sunni and Twelver are from each other). Druze (Lebanon, Syria, Israel; began as an Ismaili offshoot, now distinct). Sufism (the mystical tradition; cuts across Sunni and Shia, includes orders like the Naqshbandi, Mevlevi/Whirling Dervishes, Chishti, Qadiri). Ahmadiyya (a 19th-century Indian movement considered heretical by mainstream Sunni Islam). Nation of Islam (Elijah Muhammad, Louis Farrakhan; an American Black-nationalist movement that mainstream Sunni Islam considers heretical). Wahhabism (the Saudi reform movement, dominant in Saudi Arabia). Salafism (the broader purist movement). Deobandi (South Asian reform tradition). Barelvi (South Asian devotional tradition). The Quran (their scripture). Hadith (collected sayings of Muhammad; Sahih Bukhari and Sahih Muslim are the most authoritative Sunni collections). Sharia (Islamic law).

**Founder:** Muhammad (570-632 AD), Mecca and Medina. in Arabia, after claiming revelations from the angel Gabriel.

**Note on placement:** Islam is not historically a "Christian heresy" in the strict sense; it is its own religion with its own scriptures and prophet. However, because it makes specific claims about following the God of Abraham and of Jesus (denying His deity, denying His crucifixion, denying His resurrection) and presents itself as the final correction of Christianity, Christians have historically engaged with Islam as a post-Christian heresy. John of Damascus (c. 749 AD) classified it as a Christian heresy in his *Concerning Heresies*. Modern Islamic studies treat it as its own tradition; for the purposes of this document of flase doctrine, it belongs in this list.

**Core teachings about Jesus (Isa in Arabic):**

- Jesus is a prophet, not God.
- Jesus is not the eternal Son of God.
- The Trinity is rejected as false.
- Jesus was not crucified; God took Him directly to heaven and another (often identified as Judas) was crucified in His place (Quran, Surah 4:157-158).
- Jesus did not rise from the dead because He did not die.
- Jesus was not divine; calling Him "Son of God" is the gravest sin (*shirk*, association of partners with God; Surah 5:72-75).
- Muhammad is treated as the final prophet.
- The Quran is treated as the final revelation that "corrects" the Bible.

**Why these claims contradict the historical and biblical record:**

- The crucifixion of Jesus is one of the most attested events in ancient history. Roman, Jewish, and Christian sources independently confirm it: Tacitus (*Annals* 15.44, c. 116 AD), Josephus (*Antiquities* 18.3.3, with the disputed *Testimonium Flavianum* and the undisputed reference in 20.9.1), the Babylonian Talmud (Sanhedrin 43a), and the New Testament documents written within decades of the event by people who knew eyewitnesses. To deny the crucifixion is to deny one of the best-attested events in antiquity.
- (1 Corinthians 15:3-8): Paul's pre-Pauline creed, dating to within five years of the crucifixion, lists eyewitnesses to the resurrection by name, "most of whom are still alive" at the time of writing. This is contemporaneous testimony.
- (John 1:1, 18, 8:58, 10:30, 20:28): Jesus claimed deity. Either He was who He said He was, or He was a deluded blasphemer. He cannot be merely "a prophet" while making the claims He made.
- (Galatians 1:8): "Even if we or an angel from heaven should preach to you a gospel contrary to the one we preached to you, let him be accursed." Muhammad's claim that the angel Gabriel delivered a corrective revelation (a different gospel from Paul's) is precisely what this verse anticipates.

**The Islamic dilemma:** The Quran creates a serious problem for Islam. It says Allah gave the Torah and the Gospel. It says the Gospel contained guidance and light. It tells the People of the Gospel to judge by what Allah revealed in it. It also says no one can change Allah's words.

That leaves only two options:

1. If the Torah and Gospel were reliable in Muhammad's day, Islam is false because the Gospel teaches the death, resurrection, and deity of Christ.

2. If the Torah and Gospel were already corrupted in Muhammad's day, the Quran is false because it tells Christians to judge by corrupted books and claims to confirm revelations that no longer existed in trustworthy form.

Either way, Islam cannot escape the problem. Islam is False. It must either trust the Gospel and lose its denial of Christ, or reject the Gospel and accuse the Quran of confirming corrupted Scripture.

**Why it is false doctrine:**

- Islam denies the Son: (1 John 2:22-23): "No one who denies the Son has the Father."
- Islam denies the crucifixion: (1 Corinthians 15:3): Christ died for our sins according to the Scriptures.
- Islam denies the resurrection: (1 Corinthians 15:14): "If Christ has not been raised, then our preaching is in vain and your faith is in vain."
- Islam denies the gospel of grace: Salvation in Islam depends on submission, works, judgment, and Allah's mercy, not the finished atonement of Christ.
- Islam claims an angel delivered a later corrective revelation. (Galatians 1:8): "Even if we or an angel from heaven should preach to you a gospel contrary to the one we preached to you, let him be accursed."

**The Quran's claim that the Bible is corrupted:** Muslim apologists argue the Bible was corrupted before Muhammad, requiring the Quran's correction. The textual evidence is against this. The Dead Sea Scrolls (200 BC-100 AD), the Septuagint (3rd-2nd century BC), and the early New Testament papyri (Rylands P52, c. 125 AD; the Bodmer papyri, 2nd century) predate Muhammad by centuries and match the texts Christians have today. There is no historical period when the Bible could have been substantially altered without leaving textual traces, and the manuscripts before Muhammad show the same texts we have now.

**Why it is in this list:** Islam denies the deity of Christ, the crucifixion of Christ, the resurrection of Christ, and the sufficiency of Scripture. These are the central claims of the gospel. To Muslims who are reading: this is not personal hostility. Christians who write critically about Islam do so because the question of who Jesus is matters more than diplomatic niceties. If Jesus is who He claimed to be (and the historical evidence overwhelmingly supports the early, eyewitness testimony to His deity, death, and resurrection), then Islam is wrong about the most important question a human being can answer. (Acts 4:12).

### Hebrew Roots, Sacred Name, and Torah-Observance-as-Salvation Movements

**Also known as:** Hebrew Roots. Torah Observant Christianity. Messianic Torah movement. Sacred Name movement. Yahweh-only movement. Yahshua-only language groups. Two-House theology. Ephraimite movement. Nazarene Israel. Black Hebrew Israelite overlap. Israelite identity movements. Lunar Sabbath groups. Feast-keeping as salvation teaching.

**Note:** Jewish believers in Jesus and biblically faithful Messianic congregations are not the target of this critique. The apostles were Jewish. Jesus was Jewish. The danger begins when Torah observance becomes a requirement for salvation, a badge of superiority, or a reason to condemn other believers.

**Core distortion:** These movements often teach that true believers must keep the Mosaic law, use Hebrew names for God and Jesus, observe the biblical feasts, avoid "pagan" Christian terminology, reject Sunday worship, and sometimes reject the name "Jesus" as corrupt.

**Common phrases to recognize:**

- "Jesus is a pagan name."
- "You must say Yahshua or you are calling on the wrong name."

- "Paul has been misunderstood."
- "The law was never abolished."
- "Real believers keep Torah."
- "If you eat pork, you are disobeying God."
- "The church is Babylon."

**Why it is dangerous:** It can start with useful biblical context and end in bondage. The believer is slowly moved from Christ's finished work to calendar-keeping, food rules, Hebrew pronunciation, feast observance, and suspicion toward every ordinary Christian church.

### Monophysitism / Eutychianism

**Also known as:** Eutychianism (after Eutyches). Monophysitism (Greek: "one nature"). Miaphysitism (the related but distinct Oriental Orthodox position; "one united nature"). Non-Chalcedonian Christianity. Pre-Chalcedonian Christianity (sympathetic term used by Oriental Orthodox). Anti-Chalcedonian (polemical term). Cyrillian theology (after Cyril of Alexandria, whose language some Monophysites cited). The "one nature" school. Specific Oriental Orthodox churches that historically rejected Chalcedon: Coptic Orthodox Church, Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church, Eritrean Orthodox Tewahedo Church, Syriac Orthodox Church (formerly Jacobite), Armenian Apostolic Church, Malankara Orthodox Syrian Church (India). These churches now call themselves Miaphysite rather than Monophysite and the historical rupture is significantly understood as linguistic.

**Founder:** Eutyches, an archimandrite in Constantinople (mid-5th century).

**Core teaching:** After the incarnation, Christ has only one nature. The divine nature absorbed and overwhelmed the human nature, leaving Christ with a single (Greek *monos*) nature (*physis*) that was effectively divine.

**Why it is heresy:** This is the opposite error of Nestorianism. Christ's full humanity is essential. A Christ who is not fully human cannot represent humanity in atonement (Hebrews 2:14-17, Hebrews 4:15). The Council of Chalcedon (451 AD) defined the orthodox position: Christ is "one person in two natures, without confusion, without change, without division, without separation."

**Modern descendants:** The Oriental Orthodox churches (Coptic, Ethiopian, Armenian, Syriac, Eritrean, Indian Malankara) rejected Chalcedon and are sometimes called "non-Chalcedonian." Their position is technically *Miaphysite* (one united nature), which they distinguish from full Monophysitism. Theological dialogue has substantially reduced the historical separation, though canonical communion has not been restored. Most evangelical analysts treat the Oriental Orthodox churches as Christian, with the original Monophysite controversy being substantially over linguistic and conceptual differences.

### Nestorianism

**Also known as:** Nestorian Christianity. Nestorians. The Two-Nature heresy (in the dyoprosopite formulation). Antiochene Christology (the broader school from which Nestorianism emerged; not all Antiochene theologians were Nestorian). Church of the East (the historical descendant communion, sometimes called the Assyrian Church of the East; modern scholarship debates whether they were ever fully Nestorian or whether the label was imposed). Assyrian Church of the East (the modern name of the largest descendant church). Ancient Church of the East (a 1968 split from the Assyrian Church). Chaldean Catholic Church (a 16th-century split that came into communion with Rome). The "East Syriac" rite. Note: Modern dialogue between the Catholic Church and the Assyrian Church of the East (Common Christological Declaration, 1994) has substantially resolved the historical Nestorian dispute, treating it as substantially terminological.

**Founder:** Nestorius, Patriarch of Constantinople (428-431 AD).

**Core teaching:** Jesus Christ existed as two distinct persons (one divine, one human) loosely joined in one body. Mary was the mother of the human Jesus, not the mother of the divine Logos; therefore she should not be called *Theotokos* ("God-bearer").

**Why it is heresy:** Christ is one person with two natures (divine and human), not two persons. The unity of Christ's person is essential to the work of redemption. If the divine person did not truly experience human suffering and death, the cross is the work of a man only, and human suffering cannot atone for sin.

- (John 1:14): "And the Word became flesh and dwelt among us."
- (Philippians 2:6-8): The same person who was in the form of God took the form of a servant.
- The Council of Ephesus (431 AD) condemned Nestorianism and affirmed Mary as *Theotokos*. Note: this affirmation is about Christ's deity, not about Mary herself; calling Mary "God-bearer" was a way of insisting Jesus is God from conception.

### Pelagianism

**Also known as:** Pelagian Christianity. Semi-Pelagianism (the milder form, taught by John Cassian and others; condemned at the Council of Orange, 529 AD). Massilianism (after Marseilles, where Cassian's monastery was). The Pelagian Controversy. Caelestianism (after Caelestius, Pelagius's primary disciple). Free-will theology (when "free will" is pushed to deny the necessity of grace; not all free-will theology is Pelagian). Arminianism is sometimes accused by Calvinists of being semi-Pelagian, though classical Arminianism affirms prevenient grace and is not equivalent to Pelagianism.

**Founder:** Pelagius, a British monk active in Rome c. 410 AD.

**Core teaching:** Humans are born morally neutral, not in sin. Adam's sin affected Adam alone, not his descendants. Humans have full free will and can, in principle, achieve righteousness through their own effort. Grace is helpful but not strictly necessary for salvation.

**Why it is heresy:**

- (Romans 5:12-21): "Sin came into the world through one man, and death through sin, and so death spread to all men because all sinned."
- (Psalm 51:5): "Behold, I was brought forth in iniquity, and in sin did my mother conceive me."
- (Ephesians 2:8-9): "For by grace you have been saved through faith. And this is not your own doing; it is the gift of God, not a result of works, so that no one may boast."
- (Romans 3:10-12): "None is righteous, no, not one... no one does good, not even one."

Augustine's response to Pelagianism (in works like *On Nature and Grace*, *On the Spirit and the Letter*) became the foundation of the Western church's understanding of grace. The Council of Carthage (418 AD) and the Council of Ephesus (431 AD) condemned Pelagianism.

**Modern descendants:** Pelagianism survives wherever salvation is presented as fundamentally a matter of human effort, choice, or moral self-improvement, with Jesus as primarily an example or teacher rather than a savior who accomplishes what humans cannot.

### Donatism

**Also known as:** Donatist Church. Donatist Schism. Donatist Controversy. The Pure Church (their self-description). The Church of the Martyrs (their self-description, contrasting with the "traditores" who handed over Scriptures during persecution). The Circumcellions (a violent Donatist faction in 4th-5th century North Africa, militant and ascetic, sometimes called the "Agonistici" or "Soldiers of Christ"). Modern functional descendants don't carry the Donatist label, but the instinct survives in any movement that conditions church validity or sacramental validity on the moral perfection of clergy.

**Founder:** Donatus Magnus, c. 313 AD, in North Africa.

**Core teaching:** The validity of sacraments depends on the moral purity of the priest administering them. Specifically: priests who had handed over Scriptures during the Diocletian persecution (the "traditores") had forfeited their priestly office, and any sacrament they performed (baptism, eucharist, ordination) was invalid.

**Why it is heresy:** Augustine's response was decisive: the efficacy of a sacrament depends on Christ, not on the moral state of the minister. If sacraments depend on human purity, then no one can ever be sure their baptism is valid. The whole sacramental system collapses into anxiety.

- (Matthew 23:1-3): Jesus tells the disciples to follow what the Pharisees teach, even though their lives don't match. The teaching's truth doesn't depend on the teacher's purity.
- (Philippians 1:15-18): Paul rejoices that Christ is preached even by those preaching from envy and rivalry.

**Modern descendants:** Donatism appears whenever a Christian movement requires a "real" pastor to be morally perfect, when one moral failure is treated as voiding all prior ministry, when believers question the validity of their baptism because of a scandal in their pastor's life. The Donatist instinct is always pastoral: "we want pure leaders." The Donatist error is theological: "the gospel depends on the purity of the messenger."

### Marcionism

**Also known as:** Marcionites. Marcionist Christianity. Marcionite Church (which existed as a parallel network into the 5th century in some regions). The "two-God" heresy. Marcion's Gospel (his edited version of Luke). Marcion's Apostolikon (his edited collection of Pauline epistles: Galatians, 1 and 2 Corinthians, Romans, 1 and 2 Thessalonians, Ephesians [as "Laodiceans"], Colossians, Philippians, Philemon). Functional modern descendants who treat the Old Testament as obsolete or describing a different God include some streams of liberal Protestantism, hyper-dispensational movements that severely segment the testaments, and certain anti-Semitic theological currents (German Christian movement of the 1930s explicitly proposed a "de-Judaized" Bible, an ideological cousin of Marcionism).

**Founder:** Marcion of Sinope, c. 144 AD. Excommunicated from the Roman church for heresy.

**Core teachings:**

- The God of the Old Testament is an evil tyrant (the "Demiurge").
- The God of Jesus is a different, loving God previously unknown.
- The Old Testament should be rejected.
- Of the apostolic writings, only an edited version of Luke and ten of Paul's letters are valid; Marcion edited even these to remove Old Testament citations.

**Why it is heresy:** Marcionism severed the historical and theological continuity of revelation. The God of Israel and the God of Jesus are the same God. (John 5:39): "You search the Scriptures because you think that in them you have eternal life; and it is they that bear witness about me." (Luke 24:27): Jesus, walking to Emmaus, "interpreted to them in all the Scriptures the things concerning himself."

**Why it matters:** Marcion's challenge forced the early church to canonize the New Testament. The Muratorian Fragment (c. 170 AD), Irenaeus's *Against Heresies* (c. 180 AD), and other anti-Marcionite works are the earliest surviving Christian articulations of which books are Scripture. The 27-book New Testament we have today is, in part, the church's response to Marcion's truncated canon.

**Modern descendants:** Anyone who treats the Old Testament as obsolete, unworthy, or describing a different God from the New Testament is a functional Marcionite, even if they would deny the label. This appears in liberal theology (rejecting the Old Testament as primitive), in some forms of dispensationalism (over-segmenting the testaments), and in certain Hebrew-roots overcorrections (which is worth noting honestly: Hebrew-roots done badly inverts Marcionism by treating the New Testament as supplementary rather than fulfillment).

### Gnosticism

**Also known as:** Gnostic Christianity. Christian Gnosticism. Gnosis. Gnostic Sects. Specific schools and teachers: Valentinianism (Valentinus, ~100-165 AD; the most influential and intellectually sophisticated Gnostic school). Basilideans (Basilides, ~117-138 AD, Alexandria). Sethians / Sethian Gnostics (a school named for their veneration of Seth as a heavenly figure). Ophites (the "serpent-worshippers," who reread Genesis 3 as the serpent being the liberator). Cainites (a school venerating Cain, Esau, and Judas as figures of the higher knowledge). Carpocratians (Carpocrates, Alexandria, c. 130 AD; libertine variant). Marcionism (closely related; treated separately above). Manichaeism (Mani, 216-274 AD, Persia; a later, more developed Gnostic-influenced religion that influenced Augustine before his conversion). Mandaeism (a surviving Gnostic-related religion in Iraq and Iran that reveres John the Baptist, not Jesus). Modern descendants include the New Age movement, certain Theosophical streams, the Gnostic Society and contemporary "neo-Gnostic" churches (Ecclesia Gnostica, et al.), and the popular reception of Gnostic-themed fiction (The Da Vinci Code, etc.).

**Historical form and modern echo:** Ancient Gnosticism peaked in the second century, but the pattern never died. Modern versions appear wherever secret knowledge, hidden texts, elite enlightenment, or hostility toward the material creation replaces the plain gospel.

**Core teachings:**

- Salvation is by *gnosis* (secret knowledge), not by faith in the historic Christ.
- The material world is evil, created by a lesser god (the "Demiurge"), often identified with the God of the Old Testament.
- The true God is the unknowable, transcendent "Pleroma" (fullness).
- Jesus came to deliver secret knowledge to a spiritual elite, not to die for sin.
- The body and material existence are obstacles to spirituality.

**Major Gnostic teachers:** Valentinus, Basilides, Marcion (closely related; see above).

**Gnostic texts:** The Gospel of Thomas, Gospel of Judas, Gospel of Mary, Gospel of Philip, Pistis Sophia. Discovered in large quantities at Nag Hammadi, Egypt, in 1945. These are second- to fourth-century compositions, not apostolic, despite popular media claims to the contrary.

**Why it is heresy:**

- (1 John 1:1-3): The apostle directly refutes Docetic and Gnostic claims: "That which was from the beginning, which we have heard, which we have seen with our eyes, which we looked upon and have touched with our hands, concerning the word of life..."
- (Genesis 1:31): "And God saw everything that he had made, and behold, it was very good." Material creation is good.
- (1 Timothy 4:1-5): Forbidding marriage and certain foods (Gnostic-influenced asceticism) is named as a doctrine of demons.
- (Colossians 2:8-10): The fullness of deity dwells bodily in Christ. No secret hierarchy of aeons mediates between the Father and creation.

**Why it matters today:** Modern Gnosticism appears whenever a movement claims secret knowledge, hidden Bible codes, esoteric truth, or a privileged elite of the spiritually enlightened. The Da Vinci Code phenomenon, certain New Age teachings, and some forms of charismatic prophetic movements all carry Gnostic DNA.

### Adoptionism

**Also known as:** Adoptionist Christology. Dynamic Monarchianism (the technical patristic term). Theodotianism (after Theodotus of Byzantium, late 2nd century, an early Adoptionist excommunicated by Pope Victor I c. 190 AD). Paul of Samosata (3rd-century bishop of Antioch, condemned at the Synod of Antioch in 268 AD for Adoptionist teaching). Spanish Adoptionism (an 8th-century movement under Elipandus of Toledo and Felix of Urgell, condemned at the Council of Frankfurt in 794). Modern descendants: the Jesus Seminar's reconstruction of the historical Jesus tends toward Adoptionism. Some Liberation Theology streams. Process Theology. The "low Christology" of various 19th- and 20th-century liberal theologians (Schleiermacher in some readings, Ritschl, Harnack).

**Historical form and modern echo:** This error appears repeatedly across church history and still shows up wherever Jesus is treated as a uniquely anointed man rather than the eternal Son made flesh.

**Core teaching:** Jesus was born a fully human man, not divine. He was "adopted" as the Son of God at His baptism (Mark 1:11) or at His resurrection (Romans 1:4). His divinity is conferred, not eternal.

**Why it is heresy:**

- (John 1:1, John 17:5): The Son was eternally with the Father, before creation.
- (Philippians 2:6-7): Christ "existed in the form of God" before "taking the form of a servant."
- (Hebrews 1:3): "He is the radiance of the glory of God and the exact imprint of his nature." Not adopted; eternal.

**Modern descendants:** Some Liberation Theology and Process Theology streams recover Adoptionist patterns, presenting Jesus as a man whose deep connection with God qualifies him as "the Christ." The Jesus Seminar tradition tends in this direction.

### Docetism

**Also known as:** Docetic Christology. Phantasmal Christology. The "Seeming" heresy. Docetists. Closely overlapping with Gnostic Christology (most Gnostic schools were Docetic on the body of Christ). Specific 2nd-century teachers associated with Docetism include Cerinthus (a contemporary of the Apostle John; Irenaeus reports John fled a bathhouse where Cerinthus was bathing). Ignatius of Antioch's letters (c. 107 AD) attack Docetism repeatedly, indicating it was widespread by the early 2nd century. Some scholars argue 1 John was written specifically to refute early Docetic teaching (1 John 4:2-3 is the textbook Docetism rebuttal).

**Historical form and modern echo:** This error appeared very early, and it still shows up wherever Christ's real humanity, physical suffering, bodily death, or bodily resurrection is softened into symbol or appearance.

**Core teaching:** Jesus only *appeared* (Greek *dokein*, to seem) to have a human body. His physical form was an illusion; He did not truly suffer or die. A spiritual being of His exalted nature could not really be subject to physical limitations.

**Why it is heresy:**

- (1 John 4:2-3): "Every spirit that confesses that Jesus Christ has come in the flesh is from God, and every spirit that does not confess Jesus is not from God. This is the spirit of the antichrist."
- (1 John 1:1): "...which we have looked upon and have touched with our hands."
- (Luke 24:39): "See my hands and my feet, that it is I myself. Touch me, and see. For a spirit does not have flesh and bones as you see that I have."
- (Hebrews 2:14): "Since therefore the children share in flesh and blood, he himself likewise partook of the same things, that through death he might destroy the one who has the power of death."

If Christ did not really take on human flesh, the incarnation is fictional and the cross is theatrical. The gospel collapses.

## Fast Red Flags for False Doctrine

A movement should be tested carefully when it does any of the following:

1. **It changes Jesus.** Jesus becomes a created being, an angel, a prophet only, an enlightened master, a second god, a spirit-brother of Lucifer, or one manifestation of a one-person god.

2. **It changes the gospel.** Salvation becomes Jesus plus membership, Jesus plus rituals, Jesus plus money, Jesus plus Torah observance, Jesus plus secret knowledge, Jesus plus a prophet, Jesus plus an institution, or Jesus plus political ideology.

3. **It adds another final authority.** Scripture is no longer enough. A prophet, pope, magisterium, governing body, apostle, seer, guru, channeler, council, tradition, or new scripture becomes the real interpreter.

4. **It hides its identity.** Recruiters invite people to "Bible study," "volunteering," "English class," "campus fellowship," or "spiritual discussion" without naming the sponsoring group up front.

5. **It rewrites plain Scripture.** Every verse that contradicts the movement gets retranslated, spiritualized, dismissed as cultural, or explained away.

6. **It has failed prophecies.** The end was predicted and did not come. The leader said God spoke, and the word failed.

7. **It controls exit.** People who leave are shunned, threatened, slandered, cut off from family, or told they are leaving God Himself.

8. **It protects leaders instead of sheep.** Abuse is hidden. Victims are silenced. Leaders are untouchable. Members are told not to question "the anointed."

9. **It flatters the flesh.** It promises wealth, secret status, special bloodline identity, hidden wisdom, supernatural power, guaranteed healing, or spiritual superiority.

10. **It attacks the sufficiency of Christ.** The final test is always this: is Christ enough, or does the movement need to add something else?

## How to Test Any Teaching

The Bereans of Acts 17:11 are commended because "they received the word with all eagerness, examining the Scriptures daily to see if these things were so." They tested even the apostle Paul. You should test everyone, including this document.

When evaluating any teacher, movement, or church:

1. **Who is Jesus to them?** (1 John 4:1-3, 2 John 7-11). Fully God, fully man, eternally divine, bodily resurrected, the only mediator between God and humanity? Or something less?

2. **What is the gospel they preach?** (Galatians 1:8-9, 1 Corinthians 15:1-4). Christ died for sins according to the Scriptures, was buried, was raised on the third day, and salvation comes by grace through faith in Him? Or do they add requirements (membership, ordinances, secret knowledge, additional prophets, additional scriptures, ethnic identity, ritual observance)?

3. **What is their authority?** (2 Timothy 3:16-17). Scripture alone? Or Scripture plus a prophet, Scripture plus a magisterium, Scripture plus a Watch Tower, Scripture plus a personal revelation?

4. **What does their fruit look like?** (Matthew 7:15-20). Does the movement produce humility, holiness, love, generosity, faithful marriages, healthy children, care for the poor, courage in persecution? Or wealth concentration in leadership, sexual abuse, broken families, exploited members, predatory recruitment, and shielding of perpetrators?

5. **Do their prophecies come true?** (Deuteronomy 18:21-22). Failed predictions are disqualifying. The Watch Tower has failed five times. Mormon prophets have failed repeatedly. The Family International's apocalypses did not arrive. Failed prophecy is a closed door.

6. **Are you allowed to leave?** (John 8:32, 2 Corinthians 3:17). Cults isolate, threaten, and shun. The true church holds you with love, not with fear. If you cannot walk away from a movement without losing your family, your livelihood, and your sense of self, that movement is not the church.

7. **Do they handle Scripture honestly?** Do they let the texts say what the texts say, even when uncomfortable? Or do they retranslate, reinterpret, and explain away every passage that contradicts their position?

8. **Do they tell you their name up front?** A church or movement that recruits under one name and reveals its full doctrine and identity later has already failed the test of (2 Corinthians 4:2): "We have renounced disgraceful, underhanded ways. We refuse to practice cunning or to tamper with God's word." If a "Bible study" or "free English class" or "volunteer event" does not tell you which church or movement is sponsoring it, that is the warning sign Paul described.

9. **Do they redefine common Christian words?** False teachers often use familiar vocabulary with altered meanings. Ask what they mean by Jesus, salvation, grace, sin, repentance, resurrection, Scripture, Spirit, church, and gospel. The same word can carry a different religion under the hood.

10. **Do they make the cross insufficient?** If Christ's finished work needs to be completed by temple rites, priestly absolution, secret knowledge, new prophets, perfect law keeping, paid masses, membership cards, or loyalty to an organization, the doctrine has left apostolic ground.

11. **Do they attack the body and creation?** Scripture says creation is good and Christ came in real flesh. Teachings that treat matter, the body, marriage, food, sex within marriage, or ordinary embodied life as inherently filthy often carry Gnostic DNA.

12. **Can correction happen?** Healthy churches can repent, discipline leaders, admit error, and submit to Scripture. Corrupt systems protect the institution first and the sheep last.

If you are inside a movement listed in this document and these tests are surfacing concerns, do not panic and do not stay silent. Find a Bible-believing pastor outside your movement. Read Scripture for yourself, slowly, in context. Pray for clarity. The Holy Spirit was given precisely to lead believers into truth (John 16:13). He has done it for many before you and He can do it for you.

## Final Word

The pattern across every false doctrine in this list is brutally consistent. Someone offers what Scripture does not promise: special status, secret knowledge, guaranteed prosperity, an exclusive community, a new prophet, a corrected Bible, a softer gospel, or a shortcut around the offense of the cross. The promise feels good. That is why it works. It is still a lie.

The gospel of Jesus Christ is offensive in its simplicity. You are a sinner. Christ died for sinners. He was buried. He rose again. You are saved by trusting Him, not by what you do, join, pay, decode, inherit, or perform (Romans 3:23-24, 1 Corinthians 15:3-4, Ephesians 2:8-9). It does not flatter you. It does not make you an elite believer above the poor soul next to you. It does not promise wealth in this life. It does not require secret passwords. It is the same gospel for the apostle Paul and for a dying thief on a cross next to Jesus.

Hold fast to the faith once for all delivered to the saints (Jude 3). Test every teaching. Read the Word for yourself.

(Galatians 1:8-9): "But even if we or an angel from heaven should preach to you a gospel contrary to the one we preached to you, let him be accursed. As we have said before, so now I say again: If anyone is preaching to you a gospel contrary to the one you received, let him be accursed."
