# Revelation End-Times Timeline

This timeline follows the **straightforward futurist, premillennial reading** of Revelation as its primary framework: Christ visibly returns in Revelation 19, Satan is then bound, the saints reign with Christ for one thousand years in Revelation 20, Satan is released afterward, final judgment follows, and only then do Revelation 21-22 describe the New Heaven, New Earth, and New Jerusalem.

Revelation contains visions, interludes, flashbacks, and explanatory scenes, so not every chapter should be treated as though John were giving a modern stopwatch chronology. Revelation 12 is an obvious example because it reaches back to the birth and ascension of the Messiah before describing end-time persecution.

### Confidence labels

- **Explicit:** the text directly states the event or order.
- **Sequential inference:** the order follows naturally from the sequence of John's visions, but the text does not provide a calendar date.
- **Interpretive:** Christians disagree about the placement or symbolism.

## The whole sequence at a glance

**Seven churches -> throne room -> Lamb receives the scroll -> seven seals -> seven trumpets -> Beast and False Prophet -> seven bowls -> fall of Babylon -> visible return of Christ -> Beast and False Prophet defeated -> Satan bound -> first resurrection and 1,000-year reign -> Satan released -> final rebellion -> Satan cast into lake of fire -> Great White Throne judgment -> New Heaven and New Earth -> New Jerusalem -> eternal reign**

## Before the end-time judgments: Revelation 1-5

### Revelation 1: The risen Christ

John receives the Revelation of Jesus Christ. He sees the glorified Son of Man among seven lampstands.

**Key passages:** Revelation 1:1-20.

### Revelation 2-3: The seven churches

Jesus addresses seven historical churches in Asia Minor:

1. Ephesus
2. Smyrna
3. Pergamum
4. Thyatira
5. Sardis
6. Philadelphia
7. Laodicea

The letters contain real historical instruction and repeated promises "to the one who overcomes." The theory that the seven churches also represent seven successive eras of church history is an interpretation, not something Revelation explicitly states.

**Key passages:** Revelation 2:1-3:22.

### Revelation 4: The throne room

John is called up in vision and sees the throne of God, twenty-four elders, four living creatures, and unending worship.

**Important rapture note:** Pre-tribulation teachers sometimes treat "Come up here" in **Revelation 4:1** as a picture of the Church being raptured. The verse itself directly describes **John** being summoned into the heavenly vision. It is therefore typological evidence at most, not an explicit rapture statement.

### Revelation 5: The Lamb and the sealed scroll

A scroll sealed with seven seals is in God's hand. Only the Lamb, Jesus Christ, is worthy to open it.

Jesus is identified as:

- the Lion of the tribe of Judah
- the Root of David
- the Lamb who was slain

**Key passage:** Revelation 5:1-14.

## The Seven Seals

### Seal 1: White horse, conquest

**Revelation 6:1-2**

A rider on a white horse receives a crown and goes out conquering.

**Interpretive:** Futurists commonly identify this rider with conquest, deceptive peace, or an Antichrist-like ruler. Revelation 6 itself calls neither the rider "Antichrist" nor names him as the Beast.

### Seal 2: Red horse, war

**Revelation 6:3-4**

Peace is taken from the earth and people slaughter one another.

### Seal 3: Black horse, famine and scarcity

**Revelation 6:5-6**

Food becomes extremely expensive while oil and wine are spared.

### Seal 4: Pale horse, death

**Revelation 6:7-8**

Death and Hades receive authority over a fourth of the earth to kill by sword, famine, plague, and wild beasts.

### Seal 5: Martyrs cry for justice

**Revelation 6:9-11**

John sees the souls of people killed for the word of God and their testimony. They are told to rest until the number of their fellow servants who will also be killed is complete.

This establishes persecution and martyrdom before the final judgment.

### Seal 6: Cosmic upheaval and terror

**Revelation 6:12-17**

Events include:

- a great earthquake
- sun darkened
- moon appearing blood red
- stars falling
- sky receding like a scroll
- mountains and islands moved
- rulers and people hiding from God and the Lamb

They declare:

> "For the great day of Their wrath has come, and who is able to withstand it?"
>
> **Revelation 6:17**

This seal is extremely important for rapture debates because its cosmic signs resemble Jesus' description in **Matthew 24:29-31**.

## Revelation 7: Interlude before the seventh seal

### The 144,000 sealed

**Revelation 7:1-8**

144,000 are sealed from the tribes of Israel, 12,000 from each listed tribe.

The text gives the number twice (**Revelation 7:4; 14:1**) and lists the tribes by name (**Revelation 7:5-8**).

### The great multitude

**Revelation 7:9-17**

John sees an innumerable multitude from every nation, tribe, people, and language before God's throne and the Lamb.

They are described as those who come out of the great tribulation.

**Rapture significance:** Pre-wrath interpreters often see this scene as evidence for a gathering of believers after the sixth seal and before the trumpet judgments. The chapter does not explicitly say, "This multitude was raptured at this exact moment," so the identification remains interpretive.

## Seal 7: The seven trumpets begin

**Revelation 8:1-6**

When the seventh seal is opened:

- heaven is silent for about half an hour
- seven angels receive seven trumpets
- the prayers of the saints rise before God
- fire from the altar is hurled to earth

On a straightforward sequential reading, the trumpets emerge from the seventh seal.

## The Seven Trumpets

### Trumpet 1: Earth and vegetation struck

**Revelation 8:7**

Hail and fire mixed with blood fall upon the earth. A third of the earth and trees are burned, along with all green grass.

### Trumpet 2: Sea struck

**Revelation 8:8-9**

Something like a great burning mountain is thrown into the sea.

- a third of the sea becomes blood
- a third of sea creatures die
- a third of ships are destroyed

### Trumpet 3: Wormwood

**Revelation 8:10-11**

A great star called Wormwood falls on a third of the rivers and springs. The waters become bitter and many people die from them.

### Trumpet 4: Heavenly lights darkened

**Revelation 8:12-13**

A third of the sun, moon, and stars are struck so that a third of their light is darkened.

An eagle then announces three coming woes corresponding to the final three trumpets.

### Trumpet 5: First woe, Abyss opened

**Revelation 9:1-12**

A fallen star receives the key to the Abyss. Locust-like beings emerge and torment people who do not have God's seal for five months.

Their king is the angel of the Abyss, called **Abaddon** in Hebrew and **Apollyon** in Greek.

### Trumpet 6: Second woe, Euphrates army

**Revelation 9:13-21**

Four angels bound at the Euphrates are released. An enormous mounted force is described. A third of mankind is killed by the plagues associated with them.

The survivors still refuse to repent of idolatry, murder, sorcery, sexual immorality, and theft.

## Revelation 10: The little scroll

**Revelation 10:1-11**

A mighty angel appears with a little scroll. John is told to eat it. It is sweet in his mouth and bitter in his stomach.

John is then told that he must prophesy again about many peoples, nations, languages, and kings.

This is an interlude, not another numbered trumpet.

## Revelation 11: Temple, two witnesses, seventh trumpet

### The holy city and 42 months

**Revelation 11:1-2**

The nations trample the holy city for **42 months**.

### The two witnesses and 1,260 days

**Revelation 11:3-13**

Two witnesses prophesy for **1,260 days**.

They are eventually killed by the Beast from the Abyss. Their bodies lie exposed for three and a half days. God raises them, and they ascend to heaven as their enemies watch.

A severe earthquake follows.

### Trumpet 7: The kingdom announced

**Revelation 11:15-19**

The seventh angel sounds:

> "The kingdom of the world has become the kingdom of our Lord and of His Christ, and He will reign forever and ever."
>
> **Revelation 11:15**

The heavenly announcement speaks of God's reign, wrath, judgment, reward, and destruction of those who destroy the earth.

**Mid-tribulation rapture significance:** Some interpreters identify Paul's "last trumpet" in **1 Corinthians 15:52** with Revelation's seventh trumpet and therefore place the rapture here. Revelation never explicitly identifies the two trumpets as the same trumpet, so this is an interpretive connection.

## Revelation 12: The woman, Messiah, and dragon

Revelation 12 demonstrates why the book cannot be read as a rigid chapter-by-chapter stopwatch.

The vision includes:

- a woman clothed with the sun
- a dragon waiting to devour her child
- the birth of a male child destined to rule the nations
- the child's being caught up to God's throne
- the woman's flight into the wilderness for **1,260 days**
- war in heaven
- Michael and his angels defeating the dragon
- Satan being cast down
- persecution of the woman and the rest of her offspring

**Revelation 12:5** clearly reaches back to the Messiah's birth and ascension, while the later verses move into persecution associated with the end.

The dragon is explicitly identified as:

> "that ancient serpent called the devil and Satan, the deceiver of the whole world"
>
> **Revelation 12:9**

The dragon attacks those who:

> "keep the commandments of God and hold to the testimony of Jesus"
>
> **Revelation 12:17**

## Revelation 13: The Beast, False Prophet, and mark

### Beast from the sea

**Revelation 13:1-10**

The dragon gives the Beast power, throne, and great authority.

The Beast:

- blasphemes God
- acts with authority for **42 months**
- wages war against the saints
- receives authority over peoples, nations, languages, and tribes
- receives worship from those whose names are not in the Lamb's Book of Life

### Beast from the earth / False Prophet

**Revelation 13:11-18**

A second beast performs signs, promotes worship of the first Beast, and causes an image of the Beast to be made.

Revelation 19 later identifies the miracle-working second figure as the **False Prophet**.

### The mark of the Beast

The second Beast requires people to receive a mark on the right hand or forehead so that no one can buy or sell without it.

The number associated with the Beast is **666**.

See **Revelation 13:16-18**.

## Revelation 14: 144,000, warnings, and harvest

Revelation 14 contains several scenes:

- the Lamb and 144,000 on Mount Zion
- an angel proclaiming the eternal gospel
- an announcement that Babylon has fallen
- a warning against worshiping the Beast or receiving his mark
- a blessing upon those who die in the Lord
- a harvest of the earth
- the winepress of God's wrath

**Key passages:** Revelation 14:1-20.

Some of these scenes appear anticipatory, showing outcomes that later chapters describe in greater detail.

## Revelation 15: Preparation for the bowls

Seven angels receive seven final plagues.

Revelation says that with them God's wrath is completed.

Those who overcame the Beast, his image, and the number of his name stand before God and worship.

**Key passage:** Revelation 15:1-8.

## The Seven Bowls of Wrath

### Bowl 1: Painful sores

**Revelation 16:1-2**

Painful sores strike those who have the mark of the Beast and worship his image.

### Bowl 2: Sea becomes blood

**Revelation 16:3**

The sea becomes like the blood of a dead man, and every living thing in the sea dies.

### Bowl 3: Rivers and springs become blood

**Revelation 16:4-7**

Fresh waters become blood. The judgment is explicitly connected with the shedding of the blood of saints and prophets.

### Bowl 4: Sun scorches mankind

**Revelation 16:8-9**

The sun scorches people with intense heat. They curse God and refuse to repent.

### Bowl 5: Darkness on the Beast's kingdom

**Revelation 16:10-11**

The Beast's kingdom is plunged into darkness. People gnaw their tongues in agony and still refuse to repent.

### Bowl 6: Euphrates dried, kings gathered

**Revelation 16:12-16**

The Euphrates dries up to prepare the way for kings from the east. Demonic spirits gather the kings of the world for battle.

The gathering place is called **Armageddon**.

Jesus inserts a warning to remain awake and ready in **Revelation 16:15**.

### Bowl 7: "It is done"

**Revelation 16:17-21**

The seventh bowl brings:

- a declaration from the throne: "It is done!"
- lightning, thunder, and a massive earthquake
- collapse of the cities of the nations
- judgment upon Babylon
- islands fleeing
- mountains disappearing
- enormous hailstones

## Revelation 17-18: Babylon explained and destroyed

### Revelation 17

John sees "Babylon the Great" portrayed as a prostitute riding the Beast. The chapter explains symbolic features of the Beast and the kings aligned with it.

### Revelation 18

Babylon falls. Kings, merchants, and sailors mourn because the economic and political system from which they profited collapses under God's judgment.

God's people are warned:

> "Come out of her, My people..."
>
> **Revelation 18:4**

Revelation gives her name as Babylon the Great (**Revelation 17:5**) and no other. The text calls her "the great city that rules over the kings of the earth" (**Revelation 17:18**), seated on "seven mountains" (**Revelation 17:9**), and "drunk with the blood of the saints" (**Revelation 17:6**). The text presents her as a corrupt, wealthy, persecuting power under divine judgment.

## Revelation 19: Marriage supper and visible return of Christ

### Heaven rejoices over Babylon's judgment

**Revelation 19:1-5**

### Marriage of the Lamb

**Revelation 19:6-10**

The marriage of the Lamb arrives and His bride has made herself ready.

### Jesus returns as King of Kings

**Revelation 19:11-16**

Heaven opens and Jesus appears on a white horse.

He is called:

- Faithful and True
- the Word of God
- King of Kings and Lord of Lords

He comes to judge and wage war in righteousness and to rule the nations.

This is the clearest visible Second Coming scene in Revelation.

### Beast and False Prophet defeated

**Revelation 19:17-21**

The Beast and kings of the earth gather against Christ.

The Beast and False Prophet are captured and thrown alive into the lake of fire.

Their armies are defeated.

## Revelation 20: The Millennial Kingdom

### Satan is bound for 1,000 years

**Revelation 20:1-3**

An angel seizes Satan, binds him, throws him into the Abyss, shuts it, and seals it so that he cannot deceive the nations until the thousand years are complete.

This is why the straightforward premillennial reading places the Millennial Kingdom **after** Christ's return in Revelation 19.

The narrative sequence is:

**Christ returns -> Beast and False Prophet defeated -> Satan bound -> 1,000-year reign**

### The first resurrection and reign with Christ

**Revelation 20:4-6**

John sees thrones and those given authority to judge. He sees the souls of those who had been beheaded for their testimony of Jesus and for the word of God, and those who had not worshiped the Beast or its image and had not received its mark.

They come to life and reign with Christ for **1,000 years**.

Revelation calls this:

> "the first resurrection"
>
> **Revelation 20:5**

Those sharing in it are priests of God and Christ and reign with Him for the thousand years.

**Rapture significance:** Post-tribulation interpreters see the placement of the "first resurrection" after Revelation 19 as strong evidence that the resurrection and gathering of believers occur at Christ's visible return. Pre-tribulation interpreters generally understand the "first resurrection" as a category or program occurring in stages, with the Church raised earlier and tribulation martyrs raised here. Revelation itself does not explicitly describe those proposed stages.

## Satan's release after the Millennium

**Revelation 20:7-10**

When the thousand years are complete:

1. Satan is released from prison.
2. He deceives the nations again.
3. Gog and Magog gather for battle.
4. They surround the camp of the saints and the beloved city.
5. Fire from heaven consumes them.
6. Satan is thrown into the lake of fire.

The Beast and False Prophet are already there, having been cast into it before the Millennium in Revelation 19.

This detail establishes that the Millennial Kingdom is **not** the final New Creation. Rebellion is still possible after the thousand years because Satan is released one final time.

## Great White Throne Judgment

**Revelation 20:11-15**

After Satan's final defeat:

- a great white throne appears
- earth and heaven flee from God's presence
- the dead stand before the throne
- books are opened
- the Book of Life is opened
- the dead are judged according to their deeds
- Death and Hades are thrown into the lake of fire
- anyone not found in the Book of Life is thrown into the lake of fire

This is the second death.

## Revelation 21: New Heaven, New Earth, New Jerusalem

Only after the Millennium, Satan's release, final rebellion, and Great White Throne does John say:

> "Then I saw a new heaven and a new earth, for the first heaven and earth had passed away..."
>
> **Revelation 21:1**

The holy city, New Jerusalem, comes down from heaven from God.

Then comes one of the central promises of Scripture:

> "Behold, the dwelling place of God is with man, and He will dwell with them."
>
> **Revelation 21:3**

In the New Creation:

- God dwells with humanity
- death is gone
- mourning is gone
- crying is gone
- pain is gone
- the former things have passed away
- God makes all things new

See **Revelation 21:1-8**.

The remainder of the chapter describes the glory of New Jerusalem.

## Revelation 22: Eternal life and the final promise

Revelation ends with:

- the river of the water of life
- the tree of life
- the throne of God and the Lamb
- God's servants seeing His face
- the curse removed
- God's servants reigning forever and ever

See **Revelation 22:1-5**.

Jesus repeatedly says that He is coming soon and gives a final invitation to come and receive the water of life.

See **Revelation 22:7, 12, 16-17, 20**.

## The Millennium and New Creation are different stages

This distinction is essential.

| Millennial Kingdom | New Heaven and New Earth |
|---|---|
| Revelation 20:1-10 | Revelation 21:1-22:5 |
| Lasts 1,000 years | Eternal |
| Satan is bound | Satan is permanently in lake of fire |
| Satan is later released | No later satanic rebellion |
| Nations still exist and can later be deceived | Nothing unclean enters New Jerusalem |
| Ends with final rebellion | No more death, mourning, crying, or pain |
| Followed by Great White Throne | Follows final judgment |

On the straightforward sequential reading, the fully manifested Millennial Kingdom has **not yet occurred**, because Revelation places it after the visible defeat of the Beast and False Prophet at Christ's return and before Satan's final release.

## Does Revelation teach a seven-year Tribulation?

Revelation repeatedly gives periods of approximately three and a half years:

- **42 months**: Revelation 11:2
- **1,260 days**: Revelation 11:3
- **1,260 days**: Revelation 12:6
- **time, times, and half a time**: Revelation 12:14
- **42 months**: Revelation 13:5

A common futurist interpretation combines these periods with the final "week" of **Daniel 9:27** and constructs a seven-year end-time period divided into two halves of approximately three and a half years each.

That may be a valid harmonization, but the text must be distinguished from the model:

**Revelation itself never contains the sentence, "The Tribulation lasts seven years."**

The seven-year framework comes from combining Revelation with Daniel.

## The Rapture: what is explicit?

The rapture itself is biblical. Its timing relative to Revelation's judgments is disputed.

Paul writes:

> "For the Lord Himself will descend from heaven with a loud command, with the voice of an archangel, and with the trumpet of God, and the dead in Christ will be the first to rise. After that, we who are alive and remain will be caught up together with them in the clouds to meet the Lord in the air. And so we will always be with the Lord."
>
> **1 Thessalonians 4:16-17**

Paul also says:

> "We will not all sleep, but we will all be changed, in an instant, in the twinkling of an eye, at the last trumpet. For the trumpet will sound, the dead will be raised imperishable, and we will be changed."
>
> **1 Corinthians 15:51-52**

The Greek verb behind "caught up" is **harpazo**, meaning to seize or catch up. The English theological term "rapture" ultimately comes through the Latin translation of this concept.

The dispute is not whether believers will be caught up to meet Christ. It is clear in scripture. The dispute is **when** this will happen.

## Potential rapture placements

### 1. Pre-Tribulation

**Placement:** Before the first seal and before the end-time tribulation period.

#### Common supporting passages

- **1 Thessalonians 4:13-18**: believers caught up to meet Christ.
- **1 Thessalonians 5:9**: believers are not appointed to wrath.
- **John 14:1-3**: Christ receives His people to Himself.
- **Revelation 3:10**: promise to keep the Philadelphia church **from the hour of testing coming upon the whole world**.
- The word "church" is not used for the earthly saints in Revelation 6-18.

#### Proposed location in Revelation

Usually **between Revelation 3 and 4**, before Seal 1.

Some use Revelation 4:1, "Come up here," as a symbolic picture of the rapture.

#### Main difficulties

- Revelation 4:1 explicitly describes John entering a vision, not the Church being removed.
- Revelation never directly says the Church was raptured before Seal 1.
- Saints are clearly present and persecuted during the later visions.
- **2 Thessalonians 2:1-4** connects Christ's coming and "our being gathered together to Him" with events including the rebellion and revelation of the man of lawlessness.
- **Matthew 24:29-31** places a trumpet and gathering of the elect "immediately after" tribulation.

Pre-tribulation interpreters answer by distinguishing the rapture from Christ's later visible return and by distinguishing the Church from other saints present during the tribulation.

### 2. Mid-Tribulation

**Placement:** Around the midpoint of the final period, often associated with Revelation 11 and the seventh trumpet.

#### Common supporting connections

- **1 Corinthians 15:52**: resurrection/change occurs at "the last trumpet."
- **Revelation 11:15**: the seventh and final trumpet sounds.
- **Revelation 11:12**: the two witnesses ascend to heaven.
- Revelation repeatedly uses 42 months and 1,260 days, suggesting a major midpoint structure.

#### Main difficulties

- Scripture never explicitly says Paul's "last trumpet" is Revelation's seventh trumpet.
- The two witnesses' ascension is explicitly about the two witnesses, not the whole Church.
- Revelation's 42-month periods are not all explicitly placed as sequential halves of one seven-year period.

### 3. Post-Tribulation / At the Visible Return of Christ

**Placement:** At the end of tribulation, associated with Christ's public appearing and immediately before the Millennial Kingdom.

#### Common supporting passages

**Matthew 24:29-31** says:

- "immediately after" the tribulation
- cosmic signs occur
- the Son of Man appears on the clouds
- a loud trumpet sounds
- angels gather His elect

**1 Thessalonians 4:16-17** describes:

- the Lord descending from heaven
- a command
- an archangel's voice
- God's trumpet
- resurrection of dead believers
- living believers caught up to meet Him

**2 Thessalonians 2:1-4** speaks together of:

- "the coming of our Lord Jesus Christ"
- "our being gathered together to Him"
- the rebellion occurring first
- the man of lawlessness being revealed

**1 Corinthians 15:23** connects resurrection with Christ's coming.

**Revelation 19-20** places Christ's visible victory over the Beast immediately before Revelation describes those killed by the Beast coming to life and calls it "the first resurrection."

#### Main difficulties

- Pre-tribulation interpreters argue this collapses passages describing deliverance from wrath into passages describing Christ's judicial return.
- Revelation does not explicitly use the phrase "caught up" in chapter 19.
- Post-tribulation interpreters must explain how believers meet Christ in the air and then relate that meeting to His descent and earthly reign.

### 4. Pre-Wrath

**Placement:** After severe tribulation and persecution, often after Seal 6, but before the trumpet and bowl judgments understood as the concentrated Day-of-the-Lord wrath.

#### Common supporting connections

- **Revelation 6:12-17**: Seal 6 brings cosmic signs and the announcement that the great day of wrath has come.
- **Matthew 24:29-31**: after tribulation, cosmic signs appear, Christ comes on the clouds, a trumpet sounds, and the elect are gathered.
- **Revelation 7:9-17**: a great multitude appears before the throne from every nation and is said to come out of the great tribulation.
- **1 Thessalonians 5:9**: believers are not appointed to wrath.

#### Main difficulties

- The Lamb Himself opens the earlier seals, so some argue God's wrath is already operating before Seal 6.
- Revelation 7 never explicitly labels the great multitude's arrival as the rapture.
- The exact division between "tribulation" caused by evil powers and "wrath" directly administered by God is interpretive.


## Rapture-position comparison

| View | Approximate Revelation placement | Main supporting texts | Main textual challenge |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pre-Trib | Before Revelation 6, often between chapters 3 and 4 | 1 Thessalonians 4:16-17; 5:9; Revelation 3:10; John 14:1-3 | Revelation never explicitly places a rapture there; Matthew 24:29-31 and 2 Thessalonians 2:1-4 appear later |
| Mid-Trib | Around Revelation 11 / seventh trumpet | 1 Corinthians 15:52; Revelation 11:15; 42-month markers | "Last trumpet" is not explicitly identified with Revelation's seventh trumpet |
| Post-Trib | At Christ's visible return, near Revelation 19 | Matthew 24:29-31; 1 Thessalonians 4:16-17; 2 Thessalonians 2:1-4; Revelation 19-20 | Requires understanding catching up and visible return as one coming rather than two separated stages |
| Pre-Wrath | After Seal 6, before trumpet/bowl wrath | Revelation 6:12-17; 7:9-17; Matthew 24:29-31; 1 Thessalonians 5:9 | Revelation 7 does not explicitly call the multitude a raptured Church; timing of wrath is disputed |


## A careful synthesis of the timing evidence

Revelation never says, "The rapture occurs before Seal 1," "at Seal 6," "at Trumpet 7," or "in Revelation 19." Any rapture timeline requires harmonizing Revelation with Jesus' Olivet Discourse and Paul's letters.

Several observations are difficult to avoid:

1. **The rapture involves resurrection.** The dead in Christ rise before living believers are caught up (**1 Thessalonians 4:16-17**).
2. **It involves a trumpet.** Both **1 Thessalonians 4:16** and **1 Corinthians 15:52** say so.
3. **Jesus describes a post-tribulation gathering of the elect with a trumpet.** See **Matthew 24:29-31**.
4. **Paul connects Christ's coming and our being gathered together to Him.** See **2 Thessalonians 2:1**.
5. **Paul says the rebellion and revelation of the man of lawlessness precede "that day."** See **2 Thessalonians 2:3-4**.
6. **Revelation 20 places a resurrection after the Beast's defeat and calls it "the first resurrection."** How that phrase relates to a proposed earlier resurrection is one of the central interpretive questions.
7. **Believers are not appointed to God's wrath.** See **1 Thessalonians 5:9**. The unresolved question is whether this means removal from the entire tribulation period or preservation from God's wrath during it.

Because of those texts, this study distinguishes **the certainty of Christ's return and the gathering/resurrection of His people** from **the disputed timing model used to place that gathering on Revelation's sequence**.

## Summary

The clearest end-time sequence in Revelation is:

**Judgment unfolds through seals, trumpets, and bowls. The Beast and False Prophet rise and persecute the saints. Babylon falls. Jesus visibly returns and defeats the Beast and False Prophet. Satan is then bound. The saints reign with Christ for one thousand years. After the thousand years Satan is released, leads one final rebellion, and is thrown into the lake of fire. The Great White Throne judgment follows. Only after that does God create the New Heaven and New Earth and bring down New Jerusalem, where His people reign forever.**

The rapture is a biblical event described especially in **1 Thessalonians 4:16-17** and **1 Corinthians 15:51-52**. Its exact placement relative to Revelation's seals, trumpets, and bowls is not explicitly numbered in Revelation and should be presented as an interpretive question rather than as though the text gives a verse saying "the rapture happens here."
