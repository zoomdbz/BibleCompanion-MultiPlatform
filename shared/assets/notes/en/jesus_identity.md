# Jesus Is Jewish

Many people seem to overlook or downplay the fact that Jesus was ethnically and culturally Jewish. Jesus of Nazareth is Jewish. This is not a later theological interpretation or a cultural label imposed on Him. It is a basic fact stated throughout the New Testament.

Christian faith begins with Israel's Messiah. Jesus was born into the people of Israel, descended from Abraham, David, and the tribe of Judah. He was circumcised according to the Law, raised in a Jewish family, kept the appointed feasts of YHWH (**Leviticus 23**), taught in synagogues and at the Temple, and was publicly identified as a Jew during His earthly ministry.

## Jesus descended from Abraham, David, and Judah

> "This is the record of the genealogy of Jesus Christ, the son of David, the son of Abraham..."
>
> **Matthew 1:1**

Matthew introduces Jesus by placing Him inside Israel's covenant history. Abraham is the patriarch of Israel, and David is Israel's royal king from whose line the Messiah was promised.

Hebrews is even more explicit:

> "For it is clear that our Lord descended from Judah..."
>
> **Hebrews 7:14**

Jesus belongs to the tribe of Judah. Revelation retains this identity after His resurrection and ascension:

> "Behold, the Lion of the tribe of Judah, the Root of David, has triumphed..."
>
> **Revelation 5:5**

Jesus does not cease to be Israel's Messiah after the resurrection.

## The New Testament directly calls Jesus a Jew

When Jesus spoke with the Samaritan woman, she said:

> "You are a Jew," said the woman. "How can You ask for a drink from me, a Samaritan woman?" (For Jews do not associate with Samaritans.)
>
> **John 4:9**

Jesus did not correct her identification. In the same conversation He said:

> "...salvation is from the Jews."
>
> **John 4:22**

Paul likewise describes the Messiah as coming from Israel according to His human ancestry:

> "Theirs are the patriarchs, and from them proceeds the human descent of Christ..."
>
> **Romans 9:5**

## Jesus was circumcised and presented according to the Law

> "When the eight days before His circumcision had passed, He was named Jesus..."
>
> **Luke 2:21**

Luke then records Mary and Joseph bringing Jesus to Jerusalem in accordance with the Law of Moses and presenting Him to the Lord.

See **Luke 2:22-24**.

This places Jesus within the covenant life of Israel from infancy.

## Jesus grew up observing Passover

> "Every year His parents went to Jerusalem for the Feast of the Passover."
>
> **Luke 2:41**

At twelve years old, Jesus traveled with His family to Jerusalem for Passover and was found in the Temple among the teachers.

See **Luke 2:41-49**.

As an adult, He continued going to Jerusalem for the appointed feasts.

- **John 2:13**: Jesus goes to Jerusalem for Passover.
- **John 5:1**: Jesus goes up to Jerusalem for "a feast of the Jews."
- **John 7:2, 10**: Jesus goes to the Feast of Tabernacles.
- **John 7:37**: Jesus publicly teaches on the last and greatest day of the feast.
- **Luke 22:7-15**: Jesus celebrates His final Passover with His disciples before the crucifixion.

## Jesus taught in Jewish religious settings

Jesus regularly taught in synagogues:

- **Luke 4:16-21**
- **Matthew 4:23**
- **Mark 1:21**
- **John 6:59**

He also taught publicly at the Temple in Jerusalem:

- **John 7:14**
- **John 8:20**
- **John 10:22-23**
- **Matthew 21:23**

His disputes with scribes, Pharisees, Sadducees, and other Jewish teachers were disputes occurring inside the world of first-century Judaism. A disagreement with Jewish leaders does not make Jesus non-Jewish any more than Paul's disagreements with Jewish leaders made Paul non-Jewish.

## Jesus was executed under the title "King of the Jews"

Pilate questioned Jesus about His kingship:

- **Matthew 27:11**
- **Mark 15:2**
- **Luke 23:3**
- **John 18:33-37**

The charge placed above Him at the crucifixion identified Him as the King of the Jews.

See **Matthew 27:37**, **Mark 15:26**, **Luke 23:38**, and **John 19:19**.

The soldiers used the title in mockery (**Matthew 27:29**). Jesus affirmed it before Pilate: "You have said so" (**Matthew 27:11**). When the chief priests asked Pilate to change the inscription, he answered, "What I have written, I have written" (**John 19:21-22**).

## The risen Jesus is still the Root and Offspring of David

At the end of Revelation, the risen and glorified Jesus says:

> "I am the Root and the Offspring of David, the bright Morning Star."
>
> **Revelation 22:16**

His resurrection did not erase His human ancestry. The eternal Son took on human flesh through Israel and remains the promised Son of David.

## Gentile believers are joined to Israel's Messiah

The New Testament does not teach Gentiles to become ethnically Jewish. It teaches that Gentiles are brought near to the God of Israel through Israel's Messiah.

Paul tells Gentile believers that they were once:

> "separate from Christ, alienated from the commonwealth of Israel and strangers to the covenants of the promise..."
>
> **Ephesians 2:12**

But through Christ:

> "you who once were far away have been brought near through the blood of Christ."
>
> **Ephesians 2:13**

Paul then says Jewish and Gentile believers are reconciled into one body and that Gentile believers are:

> "fellow citizens with the saints and members of God's household."
>
> **Ephesians 2:19**

Romans uses another image. Gentile believers are wild olive branches grafted into an existing cultivated olive tree. Paul therefore warns Gentile Christians not to boast over the natural branches.

See **Romans 11:17-24**.

## What this means for Christians

Christianity cannot be understood accurately when Jesus is detached from Israel.

Jesus is:

- the Son of Abraham
- the Son of David
- from the tribe of Judah
- the Jewish Messiah
- the King of the Jews
- the fulfillment of promises God made through Israel's Scriptures
- the Savior through whom Gentiles are brought near to the God of Israel

This does not mean Christians must adopt later rabbinic Judaism. Rabbinic traditions and the Torah are not identical things; Jesus Himself sets "the commandment of God" against "the tradition of men" (**Mark 7:8-9, 13; Matthew 15:3, 6**). It does mean that reading Jesus as though He appeared in a religious vacuum produces a distorted picture of His words, His ministry, His feasts, His covenant language, and His kingdom.

## Summary

**Jesus is Jewish.** He was born from the line of Abraham and David, descended from Judah, circumcised according to the Law, raised celebrating Passover, worshiped at the Temple, kept the appointed feasts of YHWH, and was publicly identified as a Jew. The risen Christ still calls Himself the Root and Offspring of David. Gentile Christians are not given a replacement Messiah detached from Israel; they are brought near to the God of Israel through Israel's Messiah.
