# Revelation Overview

The book of Revelation (Greek: *Apokalypsis* = "unveiling") is the only book that promises a blessing to those who read it (Revelation 1:3). It is not meant to confuse; it is meant to reveal. The key to reading it is recognizing its deep roots in the Hebrew Scriptures, the feast cycle, and first-century Jewish idioms.

---

## Structure

Revelation follows a clear outline given in 1:19:
- **"The things you have seen"**: The vision of the risen Christ (Chapter 1)
- **"The things that are"**: The seven letters to the churches (Chapters 2–3)
- **"The things that are to take place after this"**: The prophetic vision (Chapters 4–22)

### The Sevens

| Sequence | Chapters | Theme |
|----------|----------|-------|
| Seven Churches | 2–3 | The state of the church across ages |
| Seven Seals | 6–8:1 | Unfolding judgment; only the Lamb is worthy to open |
| Seven Trumpets | 8–11 | Escalating warnings to the earth |
| Seven Bowls | 15–16 | Final outpouring of wrath |

Each sequence intensifies. The seals cover broad strokes, the trumpets affect a third, and the bowls are total.

## The Feast Connection

Revelation is saturated with feast imagery. The entire prophetic narrative mirrors the fall feasts, the very feasts still awaiting their ultimate fulfillment:

### Feast of Trumpets → The Return of the King
- Revelation 1:10: "I was in the Spirit on **the Lord's day**, and I heard behind me a loud voice **like a trumpet**"
- Revelation 4:1: "Come up here, and I will show you what must take place"; a trumpet voice calling upward
- 1 Thessalonians 4:16: "The Lord himself will descend from heaven with a cry of command, with the voice of an archangel, and with the **sound of the trumpet** of God"
- 1 Corinthians 15:52: "At the **last trumpet**. For the trumpet will sound, and the dead will be raised"

The Feast of Trumpets (Yom Teruah) is the only feast that begins on a new moon, the one day when **"no one knows the day or the hour"** because the new moon had to be sighted by two witnesses. This is not a statement about God keeping secrets; it is a Hebraic idiom pointing directly to this feast. A first-century Jewish listener hearing Jesus say "no one knows the day or the hour" (Matthew 24:36) would immediately think: Yom Teruah.

### Day of Atonement → Final Judgment
- Revelation 20:11–15: The Great White Throne judgment
- Zechariah 12:10: "They will look on me, the one they have pierced, and they will mourn"
- Romans 11:26–27: "All Israel will be saved... the Deliverer will come from Zion"
- Leviticus 16: The high priest enters the Holy of Holies once a year to make atonement. Jesus entered the heavenly Holy of Holies once for all (Hebrews 9:11–12). The final application remains future.

### Tabernacles → God Dwells with Man
- Revelation 21:3: "Behold, **the dwelling place of God is with man**. He will dwell with them, and they will be his people"
- Zechariah 14:16–19: In the messianic kingdom, all nations will go up to Jerusalem to celebrate the Feast of Tabernacles
- John 1:14: "The Word became flesh and **dwelt** (*eskēnōsen* = 'tabernacled') among us"

## The Pattern: First Coming and Second Coming

The first four feasts were fulfilled at the First Coming. The last three await fulfillment at the Second Coming. The gap between them, the "summer harvest," corresponds to the church age.

| Feast | First Coming Fulfillment | Second Coming Fulfillment |
|-------|--------------------------|--------------------------|
| Passover (Nisan 14) | Crucifixion: 1 Corinthians 5:7 | - |
| Unleavened Bread (Nisan 15) | Burial: 1 Peter 2:22 | - |
| Firstfruits (Nisan 16) | Resurrection: 1 Corinthians 15:20 | - |
| Pentecost (Sivan 6) | Holy Spirit: Acts 2:1–4 | - |
| *Summer gap* | *Church age* | *Church age* |
| Trumpets (Tishrei 1) | - | Return / gathering: 1 Thessalonians 4:16 |
| Atonement (Tishrei 10) | - | Israel's repentance: Romans 11:26 |
| Tabernacles (Tishrei 15) | - | God with man: Revelation 21:3 |

The spring feasts were fulfilled **on the exact days**. There is every reason to expect the fall feasts will be as well.

## Hebraic Idioms in Revelation

Much of Revelation draws on Old Testament imagery and Jewish cultural context. Recognizing these unlocks the text:

- **"No one knows the day or the hour"** (Matthew 24:36): Feast of Trumpets idiom (new moon uncertainty). See above.
- **"Like a thief in the night"** (Revelation 3:3; 1 Thessalonians 5:2): The Temple captain (*ish har habayit*) would patrol at night. If a Levite guard was found sleeping, the captain would set his garment on fire. The guard had to "watch" and "keep his garments"; hence Revelation 16:15: "Blessed is the one who stays awake, **keeping his garments**."
- **"The marriage supper of the Lamb"** (Revelation 19:7–9): Jewish wedding customs: the groom prepares a room in his father's house (John 14:2–3), then comes for the bride at an unknown time with a shout and trumpet (1 Thessalonians 4:16). The seven-day feast parallels the seven years before the public appearing.
- **"Hidden manna"** (Revelation 2:17): The jar of manna hidden in the Ark of the Covenant (Hebrews 9:4). Jeremiah reportedly hid the Ark before the Babylonian destruction (2 Maccabees 2:4–8).
- **"The Lion of the tribe of Judah"** (Revelation 5:5): Genesis 49:9–10
- **"144,000 sealed"** (Revelation 7:4–8): Twelve tribes × 12,000. Whether literal or symbolic, the number reflects the completeness of Israel.

## How to Read Revelation

1. **Read it as a first-century Jewish text**, not a 21st-century news ticker. The symbols come from the Old Testament, not modern geopolitics.
2. **Let Scripture interpret Scripture.** Nearly every image in Revelation has a source in the Hebrew Bible: Daniel, Ezekiel, Zechariah, Isaiah, Exodus.
3. **Hold timelines humbly.** Faithful Christians disagree on whether the seals, trumpets, and bowls are sequential, overlapping, or recapitulating. The central truth is not the timeline; it is the Lamb who was slain (Revelation 5:12).
4. **Watch for the feasts.** The prophetic calendar is God's calendar. The pattern has held perfectly so far.

Revelation 22:20: "He who testifies to these things says, 'Surely I am coming soon.' Amen. Come, Lord Jesus!"
