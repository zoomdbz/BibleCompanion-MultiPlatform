# The Biblical Feasts and Two Ancient Calendars

God commanded seven feasts in Leviticus 23, each tied to Israel's agricultural cycle and each pointing forward to the work of the Messiah. These are not optional traditions; they are "appointed times" (Hebrew: *moadim*), divine appointments set on God's calendar (Leviticus 23:2).

## Two Calendars in the Second Temple Period

By the time of Jesus, two calendar systems were in use among the Jewish people:

### The Hebrew Lunisolar Calendar
The calendar used by the Temple priesthood and mainstream Judaism then and now. It follows the moon for months (new moon = new month) and adds a leap month seven times every nineteen years (the Metonic cycle) to stay aligned with the solar seasons. Month lengths alternate between 29 and 30 days. Because the new moon must be sighted, the exact start of a month can be uncertain by a day; this is why the Feast of Trumpets (Tishrei 1) was traditionally observed for two days, and why the phrase **"no man knows the day or the hour"** (Matthew 24:36) was a well-known idiom for this feast. A first-century Jewish audience would have recognized it immediately, much like "the cat's out of the bag" in English.

### The Essene Solar Calendar (364-Day)
Discovered in the Dead Sea Scrolls (particularly the Book of Jubilees and 1 Enoch), this calendar counts exactly 364 days, or 52 perfect weeks. Every feast falls on the same day of the week every year. The year begins on a Wednesday (Day 4 of creation, when the luminaries were made, Genesis 1:14–19). Scholars like Annie Jaubert have proposed that Jesus followed this calendar for His last Passover, which would place the Last Supper on a Tuesday evening, resolving the apparent conflict between the Synoptic Gospels (which say Jesus ate the Passover meal) and John's Gospel (which says the crucifixion occurred before the Temple Passover). If Jesus used the Essene calendar and the Temple used the lunisolar calendar, both accounts are accurate: Jesus kept Passover on Essene Nisan 14 (Tuesday), while the Temple Passover was on lunisolar Nisan 14 (Friday).

## The Seven Feasts

### Spring Feasts: Fulfilled at the First Coming
- **Passover** (Nisan 14): The lamb is slain. Jesus was crucified on Passover (1 Corinthians 5:7; John 19:14).
- **Unleavened Bread** (Nisan 15–21): Leaven (sin) is removed. Jesus was sinless and buried (1 Peter 2:22; 1 Corinthians 5:8).
- **Firstfruits** (Nisan 16): The first sheaf of the harvest is waved before the LORD. Jesus rose from the dead as "the firstfruits of those who have fallen asleep" (1 Corinthians 15:20).
- **Pentecost / Shavuot** (Sivan 6): Fifty days after Firstfruits. The Holy Spirit was poured out on this exact day (Acts 2:1–4). The Torah was given at Sinai on this same date (Exodus 19:1).

### Fall Feasts: Awaiting Fulfillment at the Second Coming
- **Trumpets / Yom Teruah** (Tishrei 1): The shofar blast. Points to the return of Christ: "the Lord himself will descend from heaven with a cry of command, with the voice of an archangel, and with the sound of the trumpet of God" (1 Thessalonians 4:16). This is the feast where "no one knows the day or hour."
- **Day of Atonement / Yom Kippur** (Tishrei 10): The high priest enters the Holy of Holies. Points to Israel's national repentance and final atonement: "all Israel will be saved" (Romans 11:26–27; Zechariah 12:10).
- **Tabernacles / Sukkot** (Tishrei 15–21): God dwells with His people in booths. Points to the messianic kingdom: "Behold, the dwelling place of God is with man. He will dwell with them" (Revelation 21:3; Zechariah 14:16–19).

## Other Commanded Observances

Not every divine appointment fits a month-and-day grid. Four more observances are commanded in Torah but recur too often, recur too rarely, or measure time in years rather than days:

- **Weekly Sabbath** (Leviticus 23:3): the first appointed time listed, set before the annual feasts; observed every week from Friday sundown to Saturday sundown.
- **New Moons / Rosh Chodesh** (Numbers 10:10; 28:11–15): a sacrifice and trumpet blast at the start of each month, marking God's rule over time itself.
- **Sabbatical Year / Shmita** (Leviticus 25:1–7): every seventh year the land rests; no sowing, no pruning, and debts are released (Deuteronomy 15:1–2).
- **Jubilee Year / Yovel** (Leviticus 25:8–12): after seven sabbatical cycles, the fiftieth year is proclaimed with a shofar blast; slaves go free and ancestral land returns to its family. The pattern points forward to Christ, who proclaimed "the year of the Lord's favor" in Luke 4:18–19 (citing Isaiah 61:1–2).

These fall outside the monthly grid but remain part of the full biblical calendar.

## Genesis 1:14

"And God said, 'Let there be lights in the expanse of the heavens to separate the day from the night. And let them be for **signs** and for **seasons** (*moadim*: appointed times), and for days and years.'" The heavenly bodies were created, in part, to mark God's feast calendar.
