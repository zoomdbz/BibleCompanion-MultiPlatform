# Torah, the Feasts, and Gentile Believers

The New Testament is clear about two truths that must be held together:

1. **Salvation is by God's grace through faith in Jesus Christ, not by works of the Law.**
2. **Those who belong to Christ are called to obey God.**

The difficult question is not whether obedience saves. It does not. The real question is how God's Torah functions for Jewish and Gentile disciples under the New Covenant, especially after Christ fulfilled the sacrificial system and the apostles explicitly refused to require Gentile circumcision.

Scripture does not give Christians a simple chart saying, "moral laws remain, civil laws are canceled, ceremonial laws are canceled." Those labels can be useful, but the Bible itself handles the change covenantally and command by command.

### Salvation comes by grace, not Torah observance

> "For it is by grace you have been saved through faith, and this not from yourselves; it is the gift of God, not by works, so that no one can boast."
>
> **Ephesians 2:8-9**

Paul repeatedly rejects justification by works of the Law:

- **Romans 3:20-28**
- **Galatians 2:16**
- **Galatians 3:10-14**
- **Philippians 3:8-9**

Yet Ephesians does not end at verse 9:

> "For we are God's workmanship, created in Christ Jesus to do good works..."
>
> **Ephesians 2:10**

Grace produces obedience. It does not make obedience meaningless.

### Jesus connects love with obedience

> "If you love Me, you will keep My commandments."
>
> **John 14:15**

Jesus repeats the point:

- **John 14:21**: the one who has His commandments and keeps them loves Him.
- **John 15:10**: Jesus kept His Father's commandments and tells His disciples to remain in His love through obedience.
- **Matthew 7:21**: entering the kingdom is connected with doing the Father's will.

John continues the same theology:

> "For this is the love of God, that we keep His commandments. And His commandments are not burdensome."
>
> **1 John 5:3**

Revelation describes the saints as those who have both faith in Jesus and obedience to God:

> "Here is a call for the perseverance of the saints who keep the commandments of God and the faith of Jesus."
>
> **Revelation 14:12**

The biblical order is therefore:

**Grace -> faith -> new life -> love -> obedience**

Obedience is fruit, not the purchase price of salvation.

## Did Jesus abolish the Law?

Jesus directly answered this question:

> "Do not think that I have come to abolish the Law or the Prophets. I have not come to abolish them, but to fulfill them. For I tell you truly, until heaven and earth pass away, not a single jot, not a stroke of a pen, will disappear from the Law until everything is accomplished. So then, whoever breaks one of the least of these commandments and teaches others to do likewise will be called least in the kingdom of heaven; but whoever practices and teaches them will be called great in the kingdom of heaven."
>
> **Matthew 5:17-19**

Paul likewise writes:

> "Do we, then, nullify the law by this faith? Certainly not! Instead, we uphold the law."
>
> **Romans 3:31**

So "Jesus abolished God's Law" is too crude to describe the New Testament.

At the same time, the New Testament explicitly says that the covenant, priesthood, sacrifices, and some regulations changed in Christ. Those passages cannot be ignored either.

## What actually changed under the New Covenant?

### 1. The covenant administration changed

Hebrews quotes Jeremiah's promise of a New Covenant in which God writes His laws on His people's hearts.

See **Jeremiah 31:31-34** and **Hebrews 8:8-12**.

Then Hebrews says:

> "By speaking of a new covenant, He has made the first one obsolete; and what is obsolete and aging will soon disappear."
>
> **Hebrews 8:13**

This does not mean that everything God ever commanded suddenly became evil or irrelevant. It means the Sinai covenant is no longer the covenant that constitutes God's people. The Law never justified anyone (**Romans 3:20; Galatians 3:11, 21; Acts 13:39**). The New Covenant is established in Christ's blood.

See **Luke 22:20** and **1 Corinthians 11:25**.

### 2. The priesthood changed

Jesus is not a Levitical priest. Hebrews presents Him as the eternal High Priest after the order of Melchizedek.

> "For when the priesthood is changed, the law must be changed as well."
>
> **Hebrews 7:12**

See **Hebrews 7:11-28**.

This is one of the clearest statements that "nothing at all changed" is also too simple. Something did change. The Levitical priesthood no longer mediates New Covenant access to God. Jesus does.

### 3. Repeated atoning sacrifices are fulfilled by Christ

> "And by that will, we have been sanctified through the sacrifice of the body of Jesus Christ once for all."
>
> **Hebrews 10:10**

Hebrews contrasts repeated animal sacrifices with Christ's single effective sacrifice:

- **Hebrews 7:27**
- **Hebrews 9:11-14**
- **Hebrews 9:24-28**
- **Hebrews 10:11-18**

Therefore Christians should not attempt to recreate sin offerings, Day of Atonement sacrifices, or Passover sacrifices as though Christ's atonement were incomplete.

### 4. Regulations tied to the earthly sanctuary reached their appointed reform

Hebrews describes the earthly tabernacle system and says:

> "They consist only in food and drink and special washings, external regulations imposed until the time of reform."
>
> **Hebrews 9:10**

The immediate context is the earthly sanctuary, gifts, sacrifices, washings, and regulations governing ritual access. Christ enters the greater and more perfect tabernacle and obtains eternal redemption.

See **Hebrews 9:1-14**.

### 5. Gentiles are not required to become circumcised proselytes

This point is explicit.

Acts 15 begins because some believers were teaching Gentile converts:

> "Unless you are circumcised according to the custom of Moses, you cannot be saved."
>
> **Acts 15:1**

Others argued:

> "The Gentiles must be circumcised and required to obey the law of Moses."
>
> **Acts 15:5**

Peter rejects placing this requirement upon Gentile converts and declares that both Jews and Gentiles are saved through the grace of the Lord Jesus.

See **Acts 15:7-11**.

Paul is even more explicit about a Gentile who came to Christ uncircumcised:

> "Was a man still uncircumcised when called? He should not be circumcised."
>
> **1 Corinthians 7:18**

The next verse is crucial:

> "Circumcision is nothing and uncircumcision is nothing. Keeping God's commandments is what counts."
>
> **1 Corinthians 7:19**

Paul therefore distinguishes **keeping God's commandments** from **requiring a Gentile to receive circumcision**.

Romans makes the same point from Abraham. His faith was credited as righteousness before he was circumcised, so he is "the father of all who believe but are not circumcised" (**Romans 4:9-12**), and circumcision "is a matter of the heart, by the Spirit, not by the written code" (**Romans 2:28-29**).

Titus, a Greek believer, was not compelled to be circumcised.

See **Galatians 2:3-5**.

In Galatians 5, Paul condemns receiving circumcision as a covenant requirement for justification:

- **Galatians 5:2-6**

Yet Paul later circumcised Timothy, whose mother was Jewish, for missionary reasons among Jews.

See **Acts 16:1-3**.

So the New Testament position is more precise than "circumcision is sinful." Physical circumcision is not the covenant entrance requirement for Gentile Christians, and it must never be treated as necessary for justification.

## Acts 15: What did the apostles require of Gentile believers?

Acts 15 is the central New Testament passage for this question.

James concludes:

> "It is my judgment, therefore, that we should not cause trouble for the Gentiles who are turning to God. Instead, we should write and tell them to abstain from food polluted by idols, from sexual immorality, from the meat of strangled animals, and from blood. For Moses has been proclaimed in every city from ancient times and is read in the synagogues on every Sabbath."
>
> **Acts 15:19-21**

The final letter repeats the requirements:

- abstain from things sacrificed or polluted by idols
- abstain from consuming blood
- abstain from meat from strangled animals
- abstain from sexual immorality

See **Acts 15:28-29** and **Acts 21:25**.

### What Acts 15 definitely establishes

#### Gentiles are saved without circumcision

The council directly rejects circumcision as a prerequisite for salvation and inclusion in Christ.

#### The four instructions are not the entire Christian moral code

Acts 15 does not list murder, theft, lying, hatred, coveting, honoring parents, loving neighbor, or dozens of other commands that the New Testament clearly expects Christians to obey.

Therefore the four items cannot sensibly mean, "These are the only four moral rules a Gentile Christian will ever have."

#### The four instructions are rooted in God's earlier revelation

There is a strong textual relationship between Acts 15 and laws given in **Leviticus 17-18**, especially rules concerning idolatrous sacrifice, blood, improperly killed animals, and sexual immorality. Those chapters repeatedly address both Israelites and resident foreigners.

See especially:

- **Leviticus 17:7-14**
- **Leviticus 17:15-16**
- **Leviticus 18:1-30**

This makes it difficult to argue that the Jerusalem council was simply throwing Torah away. The council itself reasons from Israel's Scriptures.

#### Acts 15:21 states a fact the text does not explain

James immediately adds that Moses is read in the synagogues every Sabbath. The text does not say why he adds it.

What the chapter does show is that the council reasons from Israel's Scriptures. James grounds the decision in the prophets (**Acts 15:15-18**, quoting **Amos 9:11-12**), and Moses continued to be read every Sabbath in the cities where Gentile believers lived.

The verse supports continued exposure to Moses, but it does **not explicitly say**, "Every Gentile must eventually take on every Mosaic command." That conclusion must be argued from the rest of Scripture rather than loaded entirely onto Acts 15:21.

## What about "one law for the native and the foreigner"?

Torah repeatedly applies the same standard to Israelites and resident foreigners.

> "The same law shall apply to both the native and the foreigner who resides among you."
>
> **Exodus 12:49**

> "The same law and the same ordinance will apply both to you and to the foreigner residing with you."
>
> **Numbers 15:16**

These texts are powerful evidence that God's holiness was not based on ethnicity.

But context matters. In Exodus 12, the foreigner who wants to eat the sacrificial Passover is specifically required to have the males of his household circumcised first (**Exodus 12:43-49**). Acts 15 and 1 Corinthians 7 explicitly refuse to make circumcision a requirement for Gentile Christians.

Therefore the resident-alien laws of the Sinai covenant cannot simply be copied into the New Covenant without asking how the apostles themselves apply them after Christ.

## Ephesians 2: Did Christ "abolish the law of commandments"?

Ephesians contains language that must be faced directly:

> "...by abolishing in His flesh the law of commandments and decrees. He did this to create in Himself one new man out of the two, thus making peace."
>
> **Ephesians 2:15**

The context is the former division between Jew and Gentile. Gentiles had been alienated from the commonwealth of Israel and strangers to the covenants, but through Christ they are brought near. Christ makes the two into one new humanity.

See **Ephesians 2:11-22**.

Paul places the sentence inside his description of the old division. Gentiles were "called uncircumcised by the so-called circumcision (that done in the body by human hands)" (**Ephesians 2:11**), and Christ "has torn down the dividing wall of hostility" (**Ephesians 2:14**).

Ephesians cannot mean, "God now has no commandments," because the same letter gives numerous commands throughout **Ephesians 4-6**, quotes the Fifth Commandment as binding on believers (**Ephesians 6:2-3**), and Paul elsewhere says faith does not nullify the Law (**Romans 3:31**).

## Food laws: are they still binding on Gentile Christians?

This is one of the areas where the New Testament evidence is more complicated than either slogan suggests.

### Evidence for food liberty

Jesus teaches that defilement comes from the heart rather than from food entering the stomach.

See **Mark 7:14-23**. The dispute in the chapter is about eating with unwashed hands (**Mark 7:2-5; Matthew 15:2**). Jesus answers by setting "the commandment of God" against "the tradition of men" (**Mark 7:8-9, 13; Matthew 15:3, 6**) and locates defilement in the heart (**Mark 7:18-23**). **Mark 7:19** ends, "(Thus all foods are clean.)" Matthew's parallel ends, "but eating with unwashed hands does not defile him" (**Matthew 15:20**). The whole context should be read rather than reduced to a slogan.

Paul writes:

> "I am convinced and fully persuaded in the Lord Jesus that nothing is unclean in itself."
>
> **Romans 14:14**

And:

> "All food is clean..."
>
> **Romans 14:20**

Paul also tells believers:

- **1 Corinthians 10:25-27**: eat what is sold in the meat market or served by an unbeliever without raising questions of conscience.
- **1 Timothy 4:3-5**: foods created by God are to be received with thanksgiving and are sanctified by God's word and prayer.

These passages provide strong evidence that the apostles did not make the Leviticus 11 food distinctions a covenant-status test for Gentile believers.

### But Acts 15 still gives food-related commands

The Jerusalem council explicitly tells Gentile believers to abstain from:

- blood
- strangled animals
- food polluted or sacrificed to idols

See **Acts 15:20, 28-29**.

Paul's later teaching about idol meat also distinguishes eating ordinary marketplace meat from knowingly participating in idolatrous worship.

See **1 Corinthians 8:1-13** and **1 Corinthians 10:14-33**.

### Other passages that bear on the question

- **Leviticus 11:44-47** and **Deuteronomy 14:2-3** ground the distinction in holiness: "be holy, because I am holy," and "You must not eat any detestable thing."
- **Isaiah 66:17**, in the same prophecy that pictures all mankind worshiping from Sabbath to Sabbath (**Isaiah 66:22-23**), says those who "eat the flesh of swine and vermin and rats" "will perish together."
- **Acts 10:14**: years after the resurrection, Peter says, "I have never eaten anything impure or unclean." Peter himself states what the vision meant: "God has shown me that I should not call any man impure or unclean" (**Acts 10:28**).
- **Romans 14:2** identifies the dispute in that chapter: one person "has faith to eat all things," another "eats only vegetables." Paul forbids judging in either direction, "for God has accepted him" (**Romans 14:3**).
- The New Testament never names Leviticus 11 in its food-liberty passages, and Acts 15 names only blood, strangled meat, and idol food.

### Best text-faithful conclusion on food

Food laws are not essential to salvation, but it is too simple to say, "The New Testament abolished every food-related command." Acts 15 itself disproves that.

The New Testament nowhere commands Gentile believers to keep Leviticus 11, and it nowhere names Leviticus 11 when it speaks of food liberty (**Romans 14:14, 20; 1 Corinthians 10:25-27; 1 Timothy 4:3-5**). Acts 15 names blood, strangled meat, and idol food. Peter explains his own vision as being about people (**Acts 10:28**), and the dispute in Mark 7 is about unwashed hands (**Mark 7:2-5**).

Leviticus 11 is a command God gave and grounded in His holiness (**Leviticus 11:44-45; Deuteronomy 14:2-3**). Scripture does not permit using its observance as a basis for justification, boasting, or condemning another believer whom God has received (**Romans 14:3-4**). One can conlude based off of **Romans 14:2** it is based up to each person's faith. 

## The Sabbath: the Fourth Commandment and Gentile Believers

The weekly Sabbath deserves separate treatment from the annual feasts because it is not introduced only as a later Temple ceremony. Scripture roots it in creation, places it inside the Ten Commandments, explicitly includes the foreigner, shows Jesus defending its proper purpose, and portrays Sabbath worship in future prophecy.

### The Sabbath begins at creation, before Israel existed

Genesis places the sanctification of the seventh day at creation itself:

> "And by the seventh day God had finished the work He had been doing; so on that day He rested from all His work. Then God blessed the seventh day and sanctified it, because on that day He rested from all the work of creation that He had accomplished."
>
> **Genesis 2:2-3**

This occurs before Abraham, Israel, Sinai, the Levitical priesthood, the Temple, or the Mosaic covenant. The Sabbath therefore has a creation foundation that cannot be reduced merely to an Israelite Temple ritual.

Jesus likewise says:

> "The Sabbath was made for man, not man for the Sabbath."
>
> **Mark 2:27**

His wording presents the Sabbath as a gift made for humanity, while answering the Pharisees' charge that His disciples were doing "what is unlawful on the Sabbath" (**Mark 2:23-24**).

### The Sabbath is the Fourth Commandment

The command is explicit:

> "Remember the Sabbath day by keeping it holy. Six days you shall labor and do all your work, but the seventh day is a Sabbath to the LORD your God..."
>
> **Exodus 20:8-10**

The command is grounded in God's own creation rest:

> "For in six days the LORD made the heavens and the earth and the sea and all that is in them, but on the seventh day He rested. Therefore the LORD blessed the Sabbath day and set it apart as holy."
>
> **Exodus 20:11**

The Fourth Commandment also explicitly includes the non-Israelite living among God's people:

> "...on which you must not do any work, neither you, nor your son or daughter, nor your manservant or maidservant or livestock, nor the foreigner within your gates."
>
> **Exodus 20:10**

The command therefore was never written as though Sabbath holiness concerned ethnic Israelites while everyone around them continued ordinary labor.

### Foreigners who join themselves to the LORD are specifically connected with Sabbath keeping

Isaiah gives one of the strongest passages for the Gentile question:

> "And the foreigners who join themselves to the LORD to minister to Him, to love the name of the LORD, and to be His servants, all who keep the Sabbath without profaning it and who hold fast to My covenant, I will bring them to My holy mountain and make them joyful in My house of prayer."
>
> **Isaiah 56:6-7**

The people in view are explicitly **foreigners who join themselves to the LORD**. Sabbath keeping is presented positively as part of their faithfulness to Him.

This does not teach salvation by Sabbath observance. It teaches that foreigners who love and serve YHWH are not pictured as excluded from His Sabbath.

### Jesus kept the Sabbath and corrected distorted Sabbath keeping

Jesus' custom was to participate in Sabbath worship:

> "As was His custom, He entered the synagogue on the Sabbath."
>
> **Luke 4:16**

When Jesus confronted Sabbath controversies, He did not announce that the Sabbath command was abolished. Instead, He corrected interpretations that treated the Sabbath as more important than mercy, necessity, or doing good.

#### The sheep in the pit: mercy and necessity are lawful

Jesus asked:

> "If one of you has a sheep and it falls into a pit on the Sabbath, will he not take hold of it and lift it out? How much more valuable is a man than a sheep! Therefore it is lawful to do good on the Sabbath."
>
> **Matthew 12:11-12**

The conclusion matters. Jesus does not say, "Therefore the Sabbath no longer matters." He says:

**"It is lawful to do good on the Sabbath."**

That establishes a necessary balance for Sabbath observance. Keeping the Sabbath does not require refusing to rescue an animal, ignoring suffering, withholding medical care, or allowing preventable harm because an act could technically be called work.

#### Priestly service could require Sabbath labor

Jesus also points out:

> "Or haven't you read in the Law that on the Sabbath the priests in the temple break the Sabbath and yet are innocent?"
>
> **Matthew 12:5**

Necessary service to God did not become sinful merely because it required labor on the Sabbath.

#### Basic care for animals continued on the Sabbath

When criticized for healing on the Sabbath, Jesus answered:

> "You hypocrites!" the Lord replied. "Does not each of you on the Sabbath untie his ox or donkey from the stall and lead it to water?"
>
> **Luke 13:15**

He again uses ordinary acts of mercy and necessity to expose a distorted application of the command.

Likewise:

> "Which of you whose son or ox falls into a pit on the Sabbath day will not immediately pull him out?"
>
> **Luke 14:5**

#### The Sabbath was made for humanity

Jesus summarizes the principle:

> "The Sabbath was made for man, not man for the Sabbath. Therefore, the Son of Man is Lord even of the Sabbath."
>
> **Mark 2:27-28**

Christ claims lordship **over** the Sabbath. The passage corrects abusive Sabbath keeping while preserving the Sabbath as something God made for human good.

A biblical Sabbath should therefore include both **holiness and mercy**:

- cease from ordinary labor and from buying and selling (**Exodus 20:10; Nehemiah 13:15-19**)
- set the seventh day apart to God (**Exodus 20:8; Isaiah 58:13**)
- worship, pray, study Scripture, rest, and gather with believers (**Leviticus 23:3; Luke 4:16**)
- allow works of mercy, necessity, healing, protection, caregiving, and genuine good (**Matthew 12:11-12; Luke 13:15-16**)
- do not turn Sabbath keeping into a rule system that punishes people for helping others (**Luke 13:14-17**)

The sheep in the pit is not an exception that destroys the command. It shows how the Lord of the Sabbath says the command is to be lived.

### Jesus still speaks of the Sabbath in a future warning

In the Olivet Discourse, Jesus tells His disciples concerning a future crisis:

> "Pray that your flight will not occur in the winter or on the Sabbath."
>
> **Matthew 24:20**

Whatever one's precise interpretation of the prophecy, Jesus speaks of the Sabbath as a meaningful category in events future to the time in which He gave the warning. That is difficult to square with the idea that He regarded the Sabbath as about to become completely irrelevant.

### The Sabbath appears in future universal worship

Isaiah describes the future order in which God makes new heavens and a new earth:

> "From one New Moon to another and from one Sabbath to another, all mankind will come to worship before Me," says the LORD.
>
> **Isaiah 66:23**

The scope is not ethnic Israel alone. The prophecy says **all mankind**.

This creates a striking biblical continuity:

**Creation** -> God blesses and sanctifies the seventh day.

**Sinai** -> the Sabbath becomes the Fourth Commandment and includes the foreigner within Israel's gates.

**Prophets** -> foreigners who join themselves to YHWH are praised for keeping the Sabbath.

**Jesus** -> keeps the Sabbath, declares Himself Lord of it, and teaches its proper merciful application.

**Future worship** -> all mankind is pictured worshiping YHWH from Sabbath to Sabbath.

### What about Colossians 2:16-17?

Paul writes:

> "Therefore let no one judge you by what you eat or drink, or with regard to a feast, a New Moon, or a Sabbath. These are a shadow of the things to come, but the body that casts it belongs to Christ."
>
> **Colossians 2:16-17**

This passage is often treated as though Paul said, "Do not keep the Sabbath." He did not say that. His explicit command is:

**"Let no one judge you."**

The context identifies who is doing the judging. Paul warns against "philosophy and empty deception, which are based on human tradition" (**Colossians 2:8**) and against regulations such as "Do not handle, do not taste, do not touch!" that are "based on human commands and teachings" (**Colossians 2:20-23**). Elsewhere Scripture places the feasts and the Sabbath in the future kingdom (**Zechariah 14:16-19; Isaiah 66:23**).

Colossians 2 therefore must be considered seriously, but it should not be paraphrased into words Paul never wrote. The verse says not to let others judge believers concerning a Sabbath; it does not explicitly say the Fourth Commandment has been erased.

### What about Romans 14:5-6?

Paul writes:

> "One person regards a certain day above the others, while someone else considers every day alike. Each one should be fully convinced in his own mind."
>
> **Romans 14:5**

The chapter's own subject is eating and abstaining (**Romans 14:2-3, 6, 14-23**), and verse 6 ties the day to that same contrast: "He who observes a special day does so to the Lord; he who eats does so to the Lord... and he who abstains does so to the Lord." Paul never names the weekly Sabbath.

Because the Sabbath is not named, Romans 14 should not be treated as an explicit repeal of the Fourth Commandment.

The certain point of Romans 14 is that believers must not despise one another or use disputed practices as grounds for condemnation (**Romans 14:3-4, 10, 13**). The chapter contains no statement that cancels the seventh-day Sabbath.

### Hebrews 4 and Sabbath rest

Hebrews says:

> "There remains, then, a Sabbath rest for the people of God."
>
> **Hebrews 4:9**

The larger passage quotes Genesis 2:2 (**Hebrews 4:4**) and uses God's seventh-day rest as the pattern for the rest His people enter by faith (**Hebrews 4:3, 10-11**). The passage cites the seventh day; it does not repeal it. Verse 9 should not carry the entire Sabbath argument by itself, but it certainly does not describe the Sabbath as worthless or hostile to Christian faith.

### A Scripture-first conclusion on Gentiles and the Sabbath

When the passages are read together, there is a strong biblical case that Gentile disciples should honor the seventh-day Sabbath as an enduring command of God, not as a means of justification but as obedience flowing from faith.

The reasons are cumulative:

- the seventh day is blessed and sanctified at creation before Israel exists (**Genesis 2:2-3**)
- Sabbath is the **Fourth Commandment** (**Exodus 20:8-11**)
- the command explicitly includes **the foreigner within your gates** (**Exodus 20:10**)
- foreigners who join themselves to YHWH are praised for **keeping the Sabbath** (**Isaiah 56:6-7**)
- Jesus keeps the Sabbath and calls Himself **Lord of the Sabbath** (**Luke 4:16; Mark 2:27-28**)
- Jesus teaches that mercy, necessity, rescue, healing, and doing good are lawful on the Sabbath (**Matthew 12:5, 11-12; Luke 13:15; 14:5**)
- Jesus still speaks of the Sabbath in a future warning (**Matthew 24:20**)
- Isaiah pictures **all mankind** worshiping YHWH from Sabbath to Sabbath in the future (**Isaiah 66:23**)

Therefore Sabbath keeping should not be presented as earning salvation, nor should it be turned into the kind of burdensome legalism Jesus rebuked. The biblical pattern is to **remember the Sabbath, keep it holy, rest from ordinary labor, worship God, and still do what mercy and genuine necessity require**.

## The LORD's appointed feasts

### They are the LORD's appointed times, not merely cultural holidays

Leviticus does not introduce the biblical feasts as human traditions or merely ethnic celebrations:

> "These are My appointed feasts, the feasts of the LORD that you are to proclaim as sacred assemblies."
>
> **Leviticus 23:2**

Again:

> "These are the LORD's appointed feasts, the sacred assemblies you are to proclaim at their appointed times."
>
> **Leviticus 23:4**

The Hebrew word **moed** means an appointed time or appointed meeting. The calendar therefore belongs first to YHWH. Israel was commanded to keep His appointed times; they were not invented by later Judaism.

### The appointed times in Leviticus 23

| Appointed time | Primary passage |
|---|---|
| Weekly Sabbath | Leviticus 23:3 |
| Passover | Leviticus 23:5 |
| Unleavened Bread | Leviticus 23:6-8 |
| Firstfruits | Leviticus 23:9-14 |
| Weeks / Pentecost | Leviticus 23:15-22 |
| Trumpets | Leviticus 23:23-25 |
| Day of Atonement | Leviticus 23:26-32 |
| Tabernacles / Booths | Leviticus 23:33-43 |

Scripture repeatedly describes these observances with language such as **"permanent statute," "throughout your generations,"** or equivalent wording. See **Exodus 12:14, 17; Leviticus 23:14, 21, 31, 41**.

That does not mean every Temple sacrifice can be reproduced anywhere on earth. Many feast commands contain two layers:

1. **the appointed day or season itself**, which God establishes; and
2. **Temple, priestly, agricultural, or sacrificial actions** that can only be performed where God authorized them.

The New Testament explicitly changes the priesthood and presents Christ's atoning sacrifice as once for all. That prevents Christians from inventing private substitutes for Temple sacrifices. It does not follow that the appointed times themselves were abolished.

## Jesus kept the appointed feasts

Jesus was raised observing Passover (**Luke 2:41-42**).

As an adult He went to Jerusalem for Passover (**John 2:13**) and Tabernacles (**John 7:2, 10**).

On the final day of Tabernacles He publicly proclaimed:

> "If anyone is thirsty, let him come to Me and drink."
>
> **John 7:37**

Before His crucifixion Jesus said:

> "I have eagerly desired to eat this Passover with you before My suffering."
>
> **Luke 22:15**

The feasts are woven directly into His ministry. Christ gives them greater meaning; Scripture never records Him declaring them evil or meaningless.

## The apostles continued to live by the feast calendar

The feast calendar remains visible after the resurrection:

- **Acts 2:1**: the Holy Spirit is poured out on Pentecost.
- **Acts 20:6**: Luke dates travel after the Feast of Unleavened Bread.
- **Acts 20:16**: Paul wants to reach Jerusalem by Pentecost.
- **1 Corinthians 16:8**: Paul says he will remain at Ephesus until Pentecost.

Paul writes to the largely Gentile Corinthian church:

> "For Christ, our Passover lamb, has been sacrificed. Therefore let us keep the feast..."
>
> **1 Corinthians 5:7-8**

Paul immediately gives the feast moral depth by contrasting the old leaven of malice and wickedness with sincerity and truth. The spiritual meaning does not require the physical appointed time to become meaningless. His wording is striking: **Christ is our Passover; therefore let us keep the feast.**

## Colossians 2:16-17 does not say the feasts were abolished

Paul writes:

> "Therefore let no one judge you by what you eat or drink, or with regard to a feast, a New Moon, or a Sabbath. These are a shadow of the things to come, but the body that casts it belongs to Christ."
>
> **Colossians 2:16-17**

This passage is often paraphrased as though Paul wrote, "Do not keep the feasts, New Moons, or Sabbaths." He did not. His explicit command is:

**"Let no one judge you."**

The surrounding passage warns about human regulations, asceticism, self-imposed worship, and commands such as **"Do not handle, do not taste, do not touch"** (**Colossians 2:20-23**). That context matters.

### "A shadow of the things to come" is future-facing language

Colossians was written **after the death and resurrection of Christ**, yet Paul describes these appointed times as:

> **"a shadow of the things to come"**

A shadow is not the body itself. Christ is the substance, center, and reality to which the appointed times point. But Paul does not say the shadow is wicked, counterfeit, or erased. More importantly, he describes it as pointing toward **things to come**.

That language fits the prophetic character of the feasts. Passover points to Christ our Passover (**1 Corinthians 5:7**); Firstfruits illuminates His resurrection (**1 Corinthians 15:20, 23**); Pentecost becomes the appointed day of the Spirit's outpouring (**Acts 2:1-4**); and Scripture still places God's calendar in future kingdom prophecy (**Zechariah 14:16; Isaiah 66:23; Ezekiel 45:21-25**).

A shadow should never replace Christ. Neither should Christ's fulfillment be turned into an argument for despising the appointed pattern that reveals Him.

### The command is against judgment, not against observance

Paul's instruction is a prohibition on being judged. Colossians 2:16 therefore cannot honestly be quoted as an explicit repeal of Leviticus 23.

The text says:

**"Therefore let no one judge you by what you eat or drink, or with regard to a feast, a New Moon, or a Sabbath."**

It does not say:

**Stop observing the feast, New Moon, or Sabbath.**

## Romans 14 does not name God's appointed Sabbaths or feasts

Paul writes:

> "One person regards a certain day above the others, while someone else considers every day alike. Each one should be fully convinced in his own mind."
>
> **Romans 14:5**

Romans 14 concerns disputes over eating, abstaining, and particular days. The chapter never identifies the **weekly Sabbath**, **Passover**, **Pentecost**, **Tabernacles**, or any other Leviticus 23 feast by name.

For that reason, Romans 14 should not be used as though Paul explicitly repealed commandments that the chapter never names. A general statement about disputed devotional practice should not silently be expanded into "God's commanded appointed times are optional" without textual evidence.

The clear principle of Romans 14 remains important: believers should not despise or condemn one another over matters of conscience. But that principle must be read alongside passages that actually name God's commandments and appointed times.

## Galatians 4 must be read in its pagan and justification context

Paul warns the Galatians:

> "You are observing special days and months and seasons and years!"
>
> **Galatians 4:10**

But the immediate context begins with former pagans:

> "Formerly, when you did not know God, you were slaves to those who by nature are not gods."
>
> **Galatians 4:8**

Paul then asks how they are "turning back to those weak and worthless principles" (**Galatians 4:9**). That makes it unsafe simply to equate "days and months and seasons and years" with YHWH's appointed times and then conclude that God's own feasts are "weak and worthless."

The larger letter also opposes circumcision and Torah observance when used as a basis of justification or covenant status. Therefore Galatians absolutely forbids turning feast observance into a means of salvation. It does **not** contain a sentence saying that Passover, Pentecost, Trumpets, Atonement, or Tabernacles were abolished.

## The appointed calendar appears again in future prophecy

This is one of the strongest reasons not to treat the feasts as temporary relics. Scripture places them **after** the first coming of Christ and in prophetic descriptions of the future reign of God.

### Tabernacles under the future reign of the LORD

Zechariah describes the LORD becoming King over all the earth:

> "On that day the LORD will become King over all the earth..."
>
> **Zechariah 14:9**

Then survivors from the nations are commanded to go up to Jerusalem:

> "...year after year to worship the King, the LORD of Hosts, and to celebrate the Feast of Tabernacles."
>
> **Zechariah 14:16**

Failure to go is followed by judgment involving lack of rain (**Zechariah 14:17-19**). The participants are not merely ethnic Jews. They are **the surviving nations**.

This is extremely difficult to reconcile with the claim that the Feast of Tabernacles was permanently abolished at the cross.

### Passover and the seventh-month feast in Ezekiel's future Temple vision

Ezekiel's closing vision includes a restored sanctuary and future worship. Within that vision:

- **Ezekiel 45:21-24** describes Passover and seven days of unleavened bread.
- **Ezekiel 45:25** describes the seven-day feast in the seventh month, corresponding to Tabernacles.
- **Ezekiel 46:1-6** again refers to Sabbaths and New Moons.

The vision uses God's appointed calendar in a future kingdom setting.

### Sabbath and New Moon in the new creation prophecy

Isaiah says:

> "For just as the new heavens and the new earth, which I will make, will endure before Me..."
>
> **Isaiah 66:22**

Then:

> "From one New Moon to another and from one Sabbath to another, all mankind will come to worship before Me," says the LORD.
>
> **Isaiah 66:23**

Again, the scope is **all mankind**.

The prophetic trajectory therefore runs forward, not backward: God's appointed rhythms are not portrayed as embarrassing leftovers that disappear from His future kingdom.

## Are Gentile Christians required to obey God's Torah?

This study's conclusion is that Gentile believers are **not saved by Torah observance**, but neither are they given permission to dismiss God's commandments merely because they are Gentiles.

Gentiles are brought near through Christ (**Ephesians 2:11-22**) and grafted into the cultivated olive tree of God's people (**Romans 11:17-24**). The New Testament does not establish one moral God for Jews and another for Gentiles.

The proper question is therefore not, "How little Torah must a Gentile keep?" It is:

**What has God actually commanded, and what has the New Covenant explicitly changed in the way those commands are carried out?**

### What Scripture clearly rejects or changes

Gentile Christians are **not** required to:

- earn salvation by Torah observance
- be circumcised in order to enter the people of God
- become Jewish proselytes as a condition of justification
- offer repeated animal sacrifices for sin
- approach God through the Levitical priesthood
- recreate Temple rites privately without the sanctuary and priesthood God appointed

Those are not loopholes around obedience. They are explicit New Covenant changes established by the apostles and Hebrews.

### What Scripture clearly retains

Gentile Christians are commanded to:

- worship the one true God
- reject idolatry
- reject sexual immorality
- love God and neighbor
- honor parents
- reject murder, adultery, theft, coveting, lying, hatred, greed, and injustice
- obey Christ
- keep God's commandments as the fruit of faith
- abstain from blood, strangled meat, and idolatrous pollution according to Acts 15
- honor the seventh-day Sabbath as holy, while following Jesus' rule of mercy, necessity, healing, rescue, and doing good
- honor YHWH's appointed annual feasts without pretending to perform Temple rites that presently cannot be performed lawfully

### Annual feasts are enduring appointed times in this study

This study does **not** treat the annual feasts as optional human traditions that disappeared at the cross. The cumulative case is:

- God calls them **His appointed feasts** (**Leviticus 23:2, 4**).
- Scripture repeatedly calls their observance lasting or permanent throughout the generations (**Exodus 12:14, 17; Leviticus 23:14, 21, 31, 41**).
- Jesus kept them (**Luke 2:41-42; John 2:13; John 7:2, 10; Luke 22:15**).
- The apostles continued to organize time around them after the resurrection (**Acts 2:1; 20:6, 16; 1 Corinthians 16:8**).
- Paul says, **"Christ, our Passover lamb, has been sacrificed. Therefore let us keep the feast"** (**1 Corinthians 5:7-8**).
- Colossians calls the calendar **"a shadow of the things to come"** after Christ's resurrection (**Colossians 2:16-17**).
- Zechariah places Tabernacles under the future worldwide reign of the LORD (**Zechariah 14:9, 16-19**).
- Ezekiel's future Temple vision includes Passover, Unleavened Bread, the seventh-month feast, Sabbaths, and New Moons (**Ezekiel 45:21-25; 46:1-6**).
- Isaiah pictures all mankind worshiping YHWH from Sabbath to Sabbath and New Moon to New Moon in the new-creation prophecy (**Isaiah 66:22-23**).

The manner of observance necessarily differs where Torah requires a functioning Temple, Levitical priesthood, sacrificial altar, or produce from the land. **The inability to perform one Temple component does not abolish the appointed time itself.**

### Food laws remain a separate question

The clean/unclean animal distinctions of Leviticus 11 should not be casually bundled together with Sabbath and the appointed feasts. The New Testament contains substantial food-liberty language in **Mark 7:14-23, Romans 14:14, 20, 1 Corinthians 10:25-27, and 1 Timothy 4:3-5**, while Acts 15 still explicitly prohibits blood, strangled meat, and idolatrous food. Peter still calls certain foods unclean years after the resurrection and explains his vision as being about people (**Acts 10:14, 28**), and the new-creation prophecy places eating swine under judgment (**Isaiah 66:17**).

For that reason, this study treats the broader Leviticus 11 question separately rather than using it to erase commandments for which the continuity case is stronger.

### Land, civil, and Temple commands require their biblical setting

Some Torah commands cannot be performed by a believer who does not possess tribal land in Israel, live under Israel's national courts, or have access to the authorized sanctuary and Levitical priesthood.

That is different from saying the command was sinful or "abolished." It means its literal administration depends on conditions God Himself specified.

A Christian living outside the land should not invent a private Temple, appoint himself a Levite, execute Israel's civil penalties, or manufacture sacrificial rites God did not authorize.

## How can a modern Christian keep the feasts without a Temple?

The biblical sacrificial system was never a do-it-yourself backyard ritual. God commanded sacrifices to be brought to the place He chose.

See **Deuteronomy 12:13-14** and **Deuteronomy 16:5-6**.

Without the Temple and Levitical priesthood, a believer should not invent unauthorized substitutes. For Christians there is an additional reason: Christ has offered the decisive sacrifice once for all.

The appointed times can still be remembered and honored in ways that do not pretend to reproduce Temple rites.

### Passover

**Primary texts:** Exodus 12; Leviticus 23:5; Luke 22:7-20; 1 Corinthians 5:7-8.

Possible modern observance:

- remember the Exodus and God's deliverance
- read Exodus 12
- remember Jesus as our Passover Lamb
- share a memorial meal
- use unleavened bread and bitter herbs if desired
- teach children the Exodus and its fulfillment in Christ
- do not attempt to recreate the Temple Passover sacrifice

### Unleavened Bread

**Primary texts:** Exodus 12:15-20; Leviticus 23:6-8; 1 Corinthians 5:6-8.

Possible modern observance:

- remove leaven and abstain from leavened bread for the seven-day appointed period
- examine one's life for the "leaven" of malice, hypocrisy, and sin
- emphasize sincerity and truth

The physical sign is meant to point beyond the pantry to the heart.

### Firstfruits

**Primary texts:** Leviticus 23:9-14; 1 Corinthians 15:20-23.

Possible modern observance:

- thank God for His provision
- celebrate Christ's resurrection as the "firstfruits" of those raised from the dead
- give generously from the first and best of one's provision

The original grain presentation was tied to Israel's land and sanctuary, so a modern believer should not pretend to perform the Temple offering.

### Weeks / Pentecost

**Primary texts:** Leviticus 23:15-22; Acts 2:1-41.

Possible modern observance:

- count the appointed period
- gather for prayer and worship
- read the Sinai account and Acts 2
- remember the giving of the Holy Spirit
- practice generosity toward the poor, reflecting Leviticus 23:22

### Trumpets

**Primary texts:** Leviticus 23:23-25; Numbers 29:1-6.

Possible modern observance:

- treat the day as sacred time before God
- rest from ordinary work
- gather for worship and prayer
- sound a trumpet or shofar if available
- study biblical trumpet imagery concerning God's appearing and resurrection

Related New Testament passages include **1 Thessalonians 4:16** and **1 Corinthians 15:52**. The symbolism is striking, although Scripture does not explicitly say those verses are the final fulfillment of the Feast of Trumpets.

### Day of Atonement

**Primary texts:** Leviticus 16; Leviticus 23:26-32; Hebrews 9-10.

Possible modern observance:

- humble oneself before God, which Scripture links with fasting (**Leviticus 23:27, 29; Isaiah 58:3, 5; Psalm 35:13**)
- confess sin
- seek reconciliation
- pray
- read Hebrews 9-10 and remember Christ's once-for-all atonement

Do not recreate the animal sacrifices. Hebrews points those sacrifices toward Christ.

### Tabernacles / Booths

**Primary texts:** Leviticus 23:33-43; Deuteronomy 16:13-15; John 7; Zechariah 14:16-19.

Possible modern observance:

- build or use a temporary shelter where practical
- eat meals there
- remember Israel's wilderness journey
- thank God for provision
- gather with family and believers
- read John 7 and the future kingdom prophecy of Zechariah 14

Tabernacles carries a powerful biblical theme: God dwelling with His people.

Compare **John 1:14** and **Revelation 21:3**.

## Conclusion

The New Testament does not present grace and obedience as enemies. **Salvation is by grace through faith in Jesus Christ. Obedience is the fruit of belonging to Him.**

Jesus says:

> "If you love Me, you will keep My commandments."
>
> **John 14:15**

The New Covenant contains real changes. Gentiles are not required to become circumcised proselytes. Christ's priesthood supersedes the Levitical priesthood as the way believers approach God. His sacrifice for sin is once for all. Temple rites cannot simply be reproduced privately in places God never authorized.

But none of those truths requires the conclusion that God's Sabbath and appointed feasts were discarded. The Sabbath is rooted in creation, written into the Ten Commandments, extended to foreigners, honored and properly interpreted by Jesus, and present in future worship prophecy. The annual feasts are called **YHWH's appointed times**, repeatedly described with enduring generational language, kept by Jesus and the apostles, interpreted through Christ, and found again in future kingdom prophecy.

Colossians 2:16-17 should therefore be read carefully. Christ is the **substance**; the appointed times are the **shadow** that points to Him and to **things to come**. A Christian must never worship the shadow instead of Christ, but Scripture does not command believers to despise or erase the pattern God established.

This study therefore takes the following position:

**Gentile believers should keep the seventh-day Sabbath and honor YHWH's annual appointed feasts as obedience flowing from faith, while recognizing the explicit New Covenant changes concerning circumcision, priesthood, atoning sacrifice, and Temple-dependent rites. Mercy and necessity remain lawful on the Sabbath, and no feast observance earns salvation.**

The governing principle is simple:

**Trust Christ completely for salvation. Obey God because you love Him. Let Christ define how His Father's commandments are lived under the New Covenant.**
