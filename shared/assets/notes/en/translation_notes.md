# Translation Notes

Hebrew terms are presented with their Greek (Septuagint) and Aramaic (Biblical/Targumic/Syriac) forms when these are clearly attested and widely recognized. Where a term is only a proper name, a unique idiom, or has uncertain/late variants, Hebrew alone is given to avoid misleading associations. This aims at accuracy while showing continuity of key words across the Hebrew Bible, the Greek Old Testament, and Jewish Aramaic traditions.  

With that said, here are the most commonly misunderstood and abused translations:

---

## Sin
**Original:** Hebrew: חֵטְא (ḥetʾ); Greek: ἁμαρτία (hamartia); Aramaic: חובא (ḥova)  

**Note:** Means *"to miss the mark, go astray."* Not only big crimes, not just an action, but any deviation from God. Includes both active rebellion and failure to do what is right.

---

## Repent / Repentance
**Original:** Hebrew: שׁוּב (shuv, "turn back"); Greek: μετάνοια (metanoia); Aramaic: תיבוּתא (tēbutha)  

**Note:** Not just feeling sorry. Hebrew emphasizes turning/returning; Greek emphasizes a change of mind/heart. True repentance = realignment with God.

---

## Fear of God
**Original:** Hebrew: יִרְאָה (yir'ah); Greek: φόβος (phobos); Aramaic: דחלא (deḥlā)  

**Note:** Not terror of abuse, but awe-filled reverence of God's holiness and authority. Motivates obedience, humility, and trust, not cowering fear.

---

## Meek
**Original:** Hebrew: עָנָו (ʿanav); Greek: πραΰς (praus); Aramaic: מַכִיך (makikh)  

**Note:** Means *"gentle, humble, submitted."* Not weakness, but strength under God's control. The meek trust God instead of forcing their own way. Jesus blesses the meek in Matthew 5:5, saying they will inherit the earth.

---

## Blasphemy (esp. against the Holy Spirit)
**Original:** Hebrew: נָקַב (*naqav*, "to curse, pierce, revile"; cf. Leviticus 24:11-16); Greek: βλασφημία (*blasphēmía*) < βλάπτω (*blaptō*, "to harm") + φήμη (*phēmē*, "speech/fame"); Aramaic: גדף (*gaddēf*, "to revile, insult"; cf. Daniel 3:29 in Targum/Syriac)  

**Note:** Root meaning = *"speech that injures, slander, contempt."* In Scripture, not casual rudeness but **deliberate contempt for God**.  

**Against the Holy Spirit:** not a careless slip but **knowingly, persistently rejecting the Spirit's witness**, attributing God's work to evil (Mark 3:28–30; Matthew 12:31–32; Luke 12:10). It's not that God is unwilling to forgive, but that the person has hardened themselves so completely against the Spirit that they will never ask or accept forgiveness. This is called "unforgivable" because the Spirit is the one who convicts and draws to repentance (John 16:8); to harden against Him is to cut off the only path to forgiveness.

**Scripture:** Leviticus 24:16; Isaiah 52:5 (LXX uses *blasphēmía* for mocking God's name); Matthew 12:31–32; Mark 3:28–30; Luke 12:10; Hebrews 10:29.

---

## Idol (Carved / Graven Image)
**Original:** Hebrew: פֶּסֶל (pesel) < root פ־ס־ל "to carve"; Greek: γλυπτόν (glypton) / εἴδωλον (eidolon); Aramaic: פסל (pesal)  

**Note:** Carved/graven image (wood/stone). Prohibited when made for worship and linked to bowing/serving.  

**Scripture:** Exodus 20:4–5; Deuteronomy 5:8–9; Deuteronomy 27:15; Isaiah 44:9–20

---

## Idol (Molten / Cast Image)
**Original:** Hebrew: מַסֵּכָה (massekah) < root נ־ס־ךְ "to pour/cast"; Greek: χωνευτόν (chōneuton); Aramaic: מסכא (maskā)  

**Note:** Molten/cast metal image, formed by pouring. Condemned when used in worship.  

**Scripture:** Exodus 32:4, 8; Deuteronomy 27:15; 2 Kings 17:16; Hosea 13:2

---

## Idolatry (child sacrifice to Molech)
**Original:** Hebrew: מֹלֶךְ (Mōlekh); הֶעֱבִיר בָּאֵשׁ (heʿevir baʾesh, "cause to pass through the fire"); Greek: Μολόχ (Moloch); διαβιβάζειν ἐν πυρί; Aramaic: מולך (molekh)  

**Note:** Explicitly condemned rituals linked to certain cults.  

**Scripture:** Leviticus 18:21; 20:2–5; Deuteronomy 12:31; 2 Kings 23:10; Jeremiah 7:31; 19:5; Ezekiel 16:20–21; Psalm 106:37–38

---

## Jealous (God)
**Original:** Hebrew: קַנָּא (qannāʾ, "jealous/zealous"); Greek: ζηλωτής (zēlōtēs, "zealous"), ζῆλος (zēlos); Aramaic: קנאה (qināʾā)  

**Note:** God's "jealousy" = covenant zeal for exclusive loyalty, especially against idolatry. Better understood as "zealous" passion for His people.  

**Scripture:** Exodus 20:5; 34:14; Deuteronomy 4:24

---

## Usury / Interest
**Original:** Hebrew: נֶשֶׁךְ (neshekh); Greek: τόκος (tokos); Aramaic: נשכא (nashka)  

**Note:** Literally *"bite."* Torah forbids charging interest to fellow Israelites in need. Prophets condemn it as oppression.  

**Scripture:** Exodus 22:25; Leviticus 25:35–37; Deuteronomy 23:19; Psalm 15:5; Ezekiel 18:8

---

## Tithe
**Original:** Hebrew: מַעֲשֵׂר (maʿăsēr); Greek: δεκάτη (dekatē); Aramaic: מעשרא (maʿsrā)  

**Note:** A tenth of crops/herds tied to the land of Israel. Not money-for-blessing but covenant provision.  

**Scripture:** Numbers 18:21–24; Deuteronomy 14:22–29

---

## Tongues (Languages)
**Original:** Hebrew: לָשׁוֹן (lashon); Greek: γλῶσσα (glōssa, NT); Aramaic: לִשָּׁן (lishān)  

**Note:** Literally *"tongue, language."* In Acts 2, the Spirit enabled believers to speak in foreign human languages understood by others, clearly known languages. In 1 Corinthians 12–14, the same word describes Spirit-given utterance that requires supernatural interpretation precisely because it is not naturally understood by the hearers (1 Corinthians 14:2: "one who speaks in a tongue speaks not to men but to God; for no one understands him, but he utters mysteries in the Spirit"). The key scriptural requirement is edification and order: tongues in the assembly must be interpreted, and if there is no interpreter, the speaker must be silent (1 Corinthians 14:26–28). Whether the utterance is a known language or a Spirit-given mystery, the standard is the same: it must build up the body, not produce confusion (1 Corinthians 14:33).  

**Scripture:** Genesis 10:5; Acts 2:4–11; 1 Corinthians 12–14; 1 Corinthians 14:26–28.

---

## Lilith (night creature; not "Adam's first wife")
**Original:** Hebrew: לִילִית (lilit); Greek: Λάμια (Lamia) / Λιλίθ (var.); Aramaic: ליליתא (lilita)  

**Note:** Appears once (Isaiah 34:14) in a ruin oracle's creature list. Later folklore about "Adam's first wife" is post-biblical and not in Genesis.

---

## Grace
**Original:** Hebrew: חֵן (ḥen, "favor"); Greek: χάρις (charis); Aramaic: טיבותא (ṭivutha)  

**Note:** Unmerited favor/kindness. God's initiative to bless, forgive, empower apart from merit.

---

## Faith
**Original:** Hebrew: אֱמוּנָה (emunah); Greek: πίστις (pistis); Aramaic: הימנותא (haymanutha)  

**Note:** Not blind belief but loyalty, trust, steadfastness. Faith = relying on God, living faithfully, clinging to promises.

---

## Righteousness
**Original:** Hebrew: צְדָקָה (tsedaqah); Greek: δικαιοσύνη (dikaiosynē); Aramaic: צדקתא (tsidqeta)  

**Note:** Covenant faithfulness, justice, right relationship. Includes generosity and restorative action.

---

## Peace / Shalom
**Original:** Hebrew: שָׁלוֹם (shalom); Greek: εἰρήνη (eirēnē); Aramaic: שלמא (shlama)  

**Note:** Not just absence of war. Wholeness, wellbeing, harmony with God, people, creation.

---

## Anointed / Messiah
**Original:** Hebrew: מָשִׁיחַ (mashiach); Greek: χριστός (christos); Aramaic: משיחא (meshicha)  

**Note:** Literally "smeared with oil." Applied to kings, priests, prophets. Not always *the* Messiah but any anointed leader.

---

## Sheol
**Original:** Hebrew: שְׁאוֹל (Sheʾol); Greek: ᾅδης (Hades); Aramaic: שאולא (Sheʾola)  

**Note:** Realm of the dead/grave. Neutral in Hebrew thought; both righteous and wicked go there until God acts.

---

## Holy
**Original:** Hebrew: קָדוֹשׁ (qadosh); Greek: ἅγιος (hagios); Aramaic: קדיש (qaddish)  

**Note:** "Set apart, consecrated." God's unique nature, not just moral perfection.

---

## Hell / Gehenna
**Original:** Hebrew: גֵּי־הִנֹּם (Gē-Hinnom, "Valley of Hinnom"); Greek: γέεννα (Gehenna); Aramaic: גֵיהִנָּם (Gehinnam)  

**Note:** Literal valley south of Jerusalem linked to child sacrifice. Became a symbol of final judgment. Distinct from Sheol/Hades.

---

## Kingdom of God
**Original:** Hebrew: מַלְכוּת אֱלֹהִים (malkhut Elohim); Greek: βασιλεία τοῦ θεοῦ (basileia tou theou); Aramaic: מלכותא דאלהא (malkutha d'alahā)  

**Note:** God's reign/rule, not geography. Present wherever His will is done, climaxing in Messiah's coming and new creation.

---

## Demonized (commonly translated "Demon-Possessed")
**Original:** Hebrew: שֵׁד (*shed*, "demon"; Deut 32:17; Psalm 106:37); Greek: δαιμονίζομαι (*daimonizomai*, passive/middle verb form, "to be acted upon by a demon"); Aramaic: שֵׁידָא (*sheda*)  

**Note:**  
English Bibles often render *daimonizomai* as "demon-possessed," but the Greek form is passive ("to be demonized / under demonic influence"). The word does not always imply full ownership or absolute control. The range of meaning includes:  
- oppressed or tormented by a demon,  
- influenced in mind or body by a demon,  
- in severe cases, controlled in speech or behavior.  

The translation "demonized" better reflects the passive sense, someone affected by demonic power to varying degrees, rather than suggesting permanent possession.  

**Scripture:**  
- Matthew 4:24: "…they brought him all the sick… and those *daimonizomenous* (demonized)…"  
- Matthew 8:28–33: the Gadarene men *daimonizomenoi* showed extreme control.  
- Luke 13:11-16: a woman "bound by a spirit" for eighteen years is healed; Jesus calls this Satan's oppression.  
- Acts 10:38: "God anointed Jesus… healing all who were oppressed by the devil."  

**Clarification:**  
- Hebrew: *shedim* (plural) = hostile spirits Israel was forbidden to sacrifice to (Deut 32:17; Ps 106:37).  
- Aramaic/Syriac: *sheda* = evil spirit.  
- Focus is on influence/oppression, not metaphysical ownership.  

---

## Satan
**Original:** Hebrew: שָּׂטָן (*satan*, "adversary, accuser"); Greek: Σατανᾶς (*Satanas*) or διάβολος (*diabolos*, "slanderer"); Aramaic: סָטָנָא (*satana*)  

**Note:**  
In Hebrew, *satan* means "adversary" or "one who opposes." Sometimes human (1 Samuel 29:4; 1 Kings 11:14), sometimes spiritual (Job 1–2; Zech 3:1–2). In Greek, the term becomes a personal title for the devil, the chief opponent of God's purposes. *Diabolos* (slanderer) is often used interchangeably.  

**Scripture:**  
- Job 1:6–12: *ha-satan* appears as accuser before God.  

- Zechariah 3:1–2: Satan resists the high priest Joshua.  

- Matthew 4:1–11: Jesus tempted by the devil (*diabolos*), called Satan.  

- Revelation 12:9: "the great dragon… that ancient serpent, called the devil and Satan, who deceives the whole world."  

**Clarification:**  
- "Satan" = adversary/accuser, later crystallized as God's enemy.  

- Not equal to God, but a created being in rebellion.  

---

## Unclean Spirit
**Original:** Hebrew: רוּחַ טָמֵא (*ruach tameʾ*, "unclean spirit"; cf. Zech 13:2); Greek: πνεῦμα ἀκάθαρτον (*pneuma akatharton*); Aramaic: רוחא מסאבא (*ruḥa mesaʾba*)  

**Note:**  
Term emphasizes ritual/moral impurity and hostile spiritual influence. In the Gospels, "unclean spirit" is synonymous with "demon." The description stresses defilement and disorder, the opposite of God's holiness.  

**Scripture:**  
- Zechariah 13:2: God promises to "remove the prophets and the unclean spirit" from the land.  

- Mark 1:23–26: Jesus casts out an "unclean spirit" in the synagogue.  

- Mark 3:30: "He has an unclean spirit" is equated with demonic accusation.  

- Luke 11:24: "When an unclean spirit goes out of a person…"  

**Clarification:**  
- Hebrew prophetic background: false worship and impurity tied to hostile spirits.  

- Greek/Aramaic usage in NT shows overlap with *daimonion*.  

- Highlights spiritual corruption, not just physical illness.  

---

## Morning Star

**Original:**

- Hebrew (Isaiah 14:12): הֵילֵל בֶּן־שָׁחַר (*Helel ben Shachar*): "Shining One, Son of Dawn"

- Greek (2 Peter 1:19): Φωσφόρος (*Phōsphoros*): "Light-bringer"

- Greek (Revelation 2:28) (Revelation 22:16): ὁ ἀστὴρ ὁ πρωϊνός (*ho astēr ho prōïnos*): "Morning Star"

- Aramaic: No direct equivalent for Isaiah 14; Syriac NT follows the Greek in calling Jesus the "Morning Star."

**Note:**

- In (Isaiah 14:12), the Hebrew word is *Helel*, which means "Shining One." It is not the same term as "Morning Star" used for Jesus in Revelation.

- The immediate context addresses the king of Babylon. However, the language transcends any human ruler, "I will ascend above the heights of the clouds; I will be like the Most High" (Isaiah 14:14), which is why Christian tradition has long read a dual reference: the human king as a type of the spiritual rebellion behind him. This parallels Ezekiel 28, where the king of Tyre is addressed in language that exceeds any mortal ("you were the anointed guardian cherub… you were on the holy mountain of God").

- Luke 10:18 ("I saw Satan fall like lightning from heaven") echoes Isaiah 14's imagery. Jesus did not quote it as a direct proof text, but the resonance is there and recognized by the church fathers.

- The word *Helel* appears only once in the Hebrew Bible and is never reused for Jesus.

- The Latin Vulgate rendered *Helel* as *Lucifer* ("light-bearer"), which later became a proper name for Satan in popular tradition. The confusion arose because the Latin word was a translation, not a title.

- In the New Testament, the title *Morning Star* is used only for Jesus.

- (2 Peter 1:19) calls him *Phōsphoros* ("Light-bringer").

- (Revelation 2:28) and (Revelation 22:16) call him *the Bright Morning Star*.

- These titles are positive, Messianic, and describe Christ's glory, light, and hope.

**Scripture:**

- Isaiah 14:12–15: "Helel ben Shachar" = Shining One, Son of Dawn (addressed to the king of Babylon; traditionally read as also typifying Satan's pride and fall).

- Ezekiel 28:12–17: The king of Tyre described in language exceeding any mortal ruler, paralleling Isaiah 14.

- Luke 10:18: "I saw Satan fall like lightning from heaven."

- 2 Peter 1:19: Jesus as *Phōsphoros* (Light-bringer).

- Revelation 2:28: Jesus as the Morning Star.

- Revelation 22:16: Jesus as the Bright Morning Star.

**Clarification:**

The Hebrew word in (Isaiah 14:12) is *Helel* ("Shining One"), which is linguistically distinct from the Greek term for "Morning Star" used for Jesus in Revelation. Satan and Jesus do not share the same title.

The passage's immediate addressee is the king of Babylon, but the church has historically recognized a deeper reference to the spiritual pride and fall behind the earthly ruler. Both readings, the historical and the typological, are attested in orthodox Christian interpretation.

Any teaching that equates Satan with the *Morning Star* title belonging to Jesus is based on a conflation of two different words in two different languages, not on the original text.

## Sexual Immorality

**Original:**  
• Hebrew: זִמָּה (*zimmah*, "lewdness, wickedness, scheming"); עֶרְוָה (*ervah*, "nakedness, indecent exposure")

• Greek: πορνεία (*porneia*, "sexual immorality, illicit sex, prostitution")

• Aramaic: זִנְיָא (*zinya*, "fornication, whoredom")

**Note:**  
*Porneia* covers unlawful sex outside God's covenant. Scripture defines immorality by **forbidden relationships and acts**, not by regulating consensual intimacy within marriage.

**Explicitly forbidden:**

• Adultery; sex with someone other than your spouse (Exodus 20:14) (Deuteronomy 5:18).

• Incest; sex with close relatives, including minors under family authority (Leviticus 18:6–18).

• Rape / coercion; forced sex of any kind, including abuse of children who cannot consent (Deuteronomy 22:25–27).

• Prostitution / exploitation, including temple prostitution (Leviticus 19:29) (Deuteronomy 23:17–18) (1 Corinthians 6:15–16).

• Bestiality; sex with animals (Leviticus 18:23).

• Homosexual acts; condemned in both Old and New Testaments (Leviticus 18:22) (Romans 1:26–27) (1 Corinthians 6:9–10).

• Sex during menstruation; forbidden in the Law (Leviticus 18:19) (Leviticus 20:18).

• Cross-dressing; wearing clothes of the opposite sex is forbidden in the Law (Deuteronomy 22:5).

**Within marriage:**

• The marriage bed is "undefiled" and honored by God (Hebrews 13:4).

• Husbands and wives belong to one another and are called to meet each other's needs (1 Corinthians 7:3–5).

• Sexual intimacy is not only allowed but blessed; Psalms describe marriage, children, and family as covenant blessings (Psalms 127:3–5) (Psalm 128:1–4), and Song of Songs celebrates marital passion as good and joyful (Song of Songs 4–7).

• Marriage intimacy is a covenant blessing, pointing to Christ and the church (Ephesians 5:25–32).

**Clarification:**

• Contraception is not directly mentioned in Scripture. The Bible never calls preventing conception a sin. The case of Onan is often misused here:

• Onan (Genesis 38:8–10): Onan was required by law to give Tamar, his brother's widow, children to carry on his brother's name. He slept with her; but he always withdrew early so she could not conceive. He used her sexually while refusing his duty. God judged him for selfishness, deception, and exploiting Tamar; not for the physical act itself, and not as a universal ban on contraception.

• Masturbation is never named explicitly in Scripture, but the act falls squarely within the biblical categories of sexual impurity (akatharsia) and failure of self-control (egkrateia); Scripture confines all sexual expression to the marriage covenant (1 Corinthians 7:2-5), commands believers to master their bodies in sanctification rather than yield to passion (1 Thessalonians 4:3-5), instructs us to flee youthful lusts rather than manage them (2 Timothy 2:22), forbids making provision for the flesh to fulfill its desires (Romans 13:14), and calls us to present our bodily members as instruments of righteousness (Romans 6:13). The silence on the specific act is not permission; it is the same silence Scripture has on pornography, which no serious believer mistakes for approval.

• Polygamy is shown in the Old Testament among patriarchs and kings and was tolerated in Israel's history, with inheritance rules given (Deuteronomy 21:15–17). But it was never endorsed as God's design. From the beginning, marriage was one man and one woman joined as "one flesh" (Genesis 2:24) (Matthew 19:4–6). Kings were warned not to multiply wives (Deuteronomy 17:17), and Solomon's downfall came from many wives (1 Kings 11:1–4). The New Testament tightens the standard: leaders must be "the husband of one wife" (1 Timothy 3:2) (Titus 1:6), reflecting the return to God's original intention.

• Divorce; Jesus taught that divorcing for reasons other than sexual immorality causes adultery (Matthew 19:9).

**Key Takeaway:**  
The Bible names adultery, incest, rape, prostitution, bestiality, homosexual acts, sex during menstruation, and cross-dressing as sexual immorality. Within marriage, intimacy is honorable and blessed, and God's design is one man and one woman in faithful union.
