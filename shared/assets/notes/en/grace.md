# Grace

**Seeing the trap:** endless wrangling over which sins count or what the Greek word means can make people miss the gospel's core. The New Testament writers themselves dealt with this problem; people debating law minutiae while missing Christ.

The truth is simple and sobering: **sin is universal (Romans 3:23).** Every one of us falls short, whether through pride, greed, anger, selfishness, or a thousand other failures. The law of God reveals how far we miss the mark, but it cannot save us; it only shows our need for rescue **(Galatians 3:24).**

The heart of the gospel is not a list of rules, but a person: **Jesus Christ.** Through Him, God extends grace we could never earn. **"For by grace you have been saved through faith. And this is not your own doing; it is the gift of God" (Ephesians 2:8–9).**

So when we read passages that name specific sins, the point is not to rank them or weaponize them, but to remind us of the bigger truth: **all of us need grace.** And grace is freely given to all who turn to Christ. **"And such were some of you. But you were washed, you were sanctified, you were justified in the name of the Lord Jesus Christ and by the Spirit of our God" (1 Corinthians 6:11).**

This is the forest beyond the trees. The gospel is not about obsessing over which sins disqualify us. It's about the astonishing fact that **Jesus qualifies us despite every sin.** That is the good news.

Grace is not a license to keep living however we want; it is the power that frees us to live differently. Jesus did not condemn the broken, but He also told them to leave their old life behind. **Real faith** is not just believing He exists, but trusting Him enough to follow, turning away from sin, learning to walk in His way, and depending on His Spirit for strength. Grace forgives our past, but it also calls us into a new future.

> **"For the grace of God has appeared, bringing salvation for all people, training us to renounce ungodliness and worldly passions, and to live self-controlled, upright, and godly lives in the present age, waiting for our blessed hope, the appearing of the glory of our great God and Savior Jesus Christ, who gave himself for us to redeem us from all lawlessness and to purify for himself a people for his own possession who are zealous for good works" (Titus 2:11–14).**

### Other Supporting Scripture

- **"Neither do I condemn you; go, and from now on sin no more" (John 8:11).**

- **"What shall we say then? Are we to continue in sin that grace may abound? By no means! How can we who died to sin still live in it?" (Romans 6:1–2).**

- **"So also faith by itself, if it does not have works, is dead" (James 2:17).**

- **"Therefore, if anyone is in Christ, he is a new creation. The old has passed away; behold, the new has come" (2 Corinthians 5:17).**

- **"He himself bore our sins in his body on the tree, that we might die to sin and live to righteousness. By his wounds you have been healed" (1 Peter 2:24).**
