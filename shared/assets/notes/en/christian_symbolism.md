# Christian Symbolism

---

## Early Christian Symbols

- **Ichthys (Fish)**  
  One of the earliest symbols. The Greek word ἰχθύς (ichthys) means "fish" and was used as an acronym for *"Ἰησοῦς Χριστὸς Θεοῦ Υἱὸς Σωτήρ”*: “Jesus Christ, Son of God, Savior.” (cf. Matthew 4:19)

- **Chi-Rho (☧)**  
  A monogram using the first two Greek letters of “Christ” (Χ and Ρ). Documented as early as the 3rd–4th century.

- **Shepherd and Sheep**  
  Depictions of Christ as the Good Shepherd were common in catacomb art (cf. John 10:11).

---

## The Cross

- In the Roman world, the **cross was an execution device**; a symbol of shame and death.  
- Paul referred to the “message of the cross” as God’s power (1 Corinthians 1:18), but early Christians did **not** immediately adopt the cross as their emblem.
- Paul also described the cross as a place of boasting in Christ (Galatians 6:14) and union with Him in death and resurrection (Romans 6:5).  
- The **shift to the cross** as the central Christian symbol developed over time, especially by the 4th century.  
- Today, the cross remains a universal emblem of Christianity, though historically it symbolizes **Christ’s death** more than His resurrection.

---

## Reflection

Early Christians used fish, Chi-Rho, and shepherd imagery before the cross became dominant. This raises the question: *is the cross the fullest symbol of Jesus, or mainly of His death?*
